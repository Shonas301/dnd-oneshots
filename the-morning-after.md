# THE MORNING AFTER
### a one-shot for D&D 2024 (5.5e)

**party:** 5 PCs, level 3, all fae or fae-touched
**runtime:** 3–5 hours
**weight:** heavy roleplay, one short combat, no dungeon, no travel
**setting:** a trampled oat field outside Secomber, in the Delimbiyr valley, at five past dawn

---

## 1. at a glance

Puck booked a festival. nine hours, one field, six hundred fae, and the whole of Secomber invited on the grounds that the ale was free and nobody reads a handbill closely at that hour. he hired the party as **crew**. it went extraordinarily well.

the party does not remember any of it. the crossing takes memory on the way in — that's the ticket price, and they paid it, and nobody warned them how much they'd want it back by breakfast.

at dawn there is a **contract** with five names on it and one signature, and the signature is a problem. a fae **claims-adjuster** is walking the field closing the ledger before the Feywild half folds up at noon. she is polite. she is on time. she will take everything.

**the secret:** the debt is real, the contract is good, and every line item on it is genuinely the party's fault. their instinct will be to prove they didn't do it. they did. the win condition is not innocence. it's accounting.

**the engine:** reverse investigation. the party reconstructs the night out of other people's accounts, and each recovered fragment is a **flashback the players get to play**, not a monologue they get to sit through. see §5. that section is the one-shot; everything else is scaffolding.

**the twist:** one PC signed on purpose, at four in the morning, knowing what it cost. they will not recover why until the last twenty minutes.

### session flow

| act | content | time |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | dawn: pockets, the Adjuster, the Ledger | 30 min |
| **II** | the survivors: witnesses, first Scraps | 75 min |
| **III** | the field: Puck's market, the wreckage, the tarp | 60 min |
| **IV** | the Encore: the one combat | 40 min |
| **V** | the Last Scrap, and noon | 35 min |

**trim to 3 hours:** run four Scraps instead of six, cut Puck's market to a single purchase, skip the sleepers. Act II is where the fat is; Act V is not.
**expand to 5:** run the counter-claim (§8), wake the sleepers, and let the party argue with Reeve Cull across the ring for as long as they like. it's free entertainment and she is very good at it.

### safety

> **session zero. two minutes. not optional.**
>
> this one-shot opens with stolen memory and closes with a betrayal committed by one of the players' own characters. neither should be a surprise to anybody.
>
> - **lines and veils, X-card or equivalent.** say it out loud and mean it.
> - **memory loss is never loss of agency here.** no PC ever acted out of character. they acted *in* character and forgot. the player decides what their character would have done, always, including retroactively. that is the entire flashback rule and it is also the safety rule.
> - **the Signatory is opt-in.** offer it privately before play (§3). if nobody wants it, there is an out that costs the story nothing.
> - **there is a body.** Hew Marrin, seventy-eight, died dancing at three in the morning of nothing worse than being seventy-eight and delighted. it isn't gory and it isn't anyone's fault, but it is the emotional center of the session. a table that doesn't want a death inside a comedy should hear about it beforehand, not at the reveal. swapping him for *collapsed, hasn't woken up* costs almost nothing.

## 2. the fold

draw eight boxes where everyone can see them. write **NOON** under the last one.

> **THE FOLD: 8 SEGMENTS**
> half of this field is going home. it is not waiting.

the field is bisected. the Secomber half is mud and oats and a rooster. the other half still has three moons up and is rolling itself away along the seam, about a foot every ten minutes, politely, the way a caterer strikes a room while people are still in it.

**advance the Fold by 1 when:**

- an Act begins (Acts II, III, and IV each burn one — three automatic)
- a Scrap spoils on a Contradiction (§5)
- Quietus catches the party in a lie (her *Noted* reaction)
- the party tries to leave the ring
- any round of the Encore fight after the third
- ten real minutes pass with nothing recovered and nobody talking to anyone new — this one is the DM's whip and you should use it

**the Fold never goes back.** not for a good roll, not for a great speech, not for anything. tell the players that in the first five minutes and let it sit there.

**pacing target:** 6 when the tarp comes off. if it hits 8 before the Encore is down, the Encore wakes anyway, at noon, in front of everyone, on ground that is actively leaving. that version is worse and it is also better. run it.

## 3. the crew

everyone is fae or fae-touched, and everyone worked this festival. that's load-bearing: it puts every PC inside every scene, it explains why their names are on a contract, and it means the answer to "were you there?" is always yes.

### suggested species (2024 PHB + expansions)
Fairy, Satyr, Harengon, Changeling, Eladrin, Goblin, Hexblood, Centaur, or any species carrying the **Fey-Touched** origin feat and a decent excuse.

### the five posts

| post | job last night | class fits |
|---|---|---|
| **the Gate** | took names at the ring. counted everyone in. | Rogue, Warlock (Archfey), Fighter |
| **the Backline** | carried, tuned, and guarded every instrument in the field. | Bard, Artificer, Ranger |
| **the Pour** | six barrels, six hundred people, nine hours. | Druid, Monk, anyone with Brewer's Supplies |
| **the Peace** | broke up the fights. there were forty. | Barbarian, Paladin, Fighter |
| **the Bill** | announced the acts. kept the running order. | Bard (Lore), Cleric, Sorcerer |

each post grants a **Standing** — a once-per-session narrative permission. not a spell. no save, no roll, no counterplay.

- **the Gate:** *"I have you in the book."* name any creature. you know exactly what it came here for. not what it did. what it **wanted**.
- **the Backline:** *"that was in my case."* produce one nonmagical object that would fit in an instrument case. it's yours, it always was, and you get to say who handed it to you.
- **the Pour:** *"you've had three of these."* name a creature that drank last night. its next d20 test is made with Disadvantage as the night comes back up on it, and it answers one question honestly before it can stop itself.
- **the Peace:** *"I pulled you out of that."* declare that you personally saved one creature from one specific thing last night. true. it owes you and knows it: Advantage on your next check against it, and it cannot refuse you a small favor.
- **the Bill:** *"that was on the bill."* declare that one event of last night was scheduled, announced, and sanctioned. the ledger agrees with you. **Quietus strikes a line item.**

the Bill's Standing settles a debt outright. that is correct. let it work, don't argue, and be pleased when they find it.

### the Signatory

> **offer this privately, before session, by text or a folded card. do not do it at the table.**
>
> *"one PC did something last night on purpose, and hid it from the others. it is not evil, it will not make your character look stupid, and you get the best scene in the session at the end of it. want it? yes / no."*

first yes takes it. **tell them nothing else.** they play the whole session genuinely not knowing what they did, which is the point and also the best seat in the house.

**if nobody says yes:** the signature is in a PC's handwriting, but the hand was Bell Orrick's. Bell asked to borrow it at four in the morning and the PC said yes without reading, because it was four in the morning and Bell was crying. every beat downstream still lands — the Signatory becomes a witness to their own kindness instead of the author of it. this is not the lesser version. do not run it apologetically.

## 4. act I: dawn in the oat field

**open here. there is no travel and there is no tavern.**

> you wake face-down in oats.
>
> not oats, exactly. what's left of oats, after six hundred people danced on them for nine hours. the mud is to the knee. the sky is the flat grey of five past dawn. somewhere east of here a rooster in Secomber is doing its job with no idea what it is waking up.
>
> there is paper folded into your shirt. there are four other people in this field you know extremely well and cannot, at this moment, entirely place. and there is a hole where last night should be. not a blur. a hole, with edges.
>
> the field is cut in half lengthwise. one half is a ruined crop in the Delimbiyr valley. the other half still has three moons up in it, and it is rolling itself away along the seam, very slowly, the way a caterer strikes a room while people are still standing in it.
>
> a woman in a wet grey coat is walking the line between the halves with a ledger open across her forearm. she is writing without looking down.
>
> "morning. don't move. I'll get to you."

### pockets

every PC rolls once on **d10: what's in your pockets** (§12). roll it in front of them. do not explain anything.

### the Adjuster

**QUIETUS, Adjuster of the Morning Circuit** (§11) works the field methodically and gets to the party fourth. she is not a villain. she is a bailiff, she is punctual, she is incorruptible, and she will take everything they have at noon without raising her voice once.

she establishes four facts, in this order, and she does not repeat herself:

1. **the ring is closed.** the oats will not let anyone in or out until the ledger closes or noon comes, whichever is first. Reeve Cull has been standing at the edge of her own field since first light and cannot take a step into it.
2. **the debt exists.** she reads the contract aloud. five names as parties of the second part. one signature. **the party is jointly liable and one of them signed.**
3. **the ledger has seven lines.** any line the party can settle, prove paid, or prove was never theirs comes off. anything still open at noon comes due in full, all at once, from whoever's standing closest.
4. **she will tell them how to beat her.** the rules require it. she does it once, quickly, in a bored voice, and if they aren't listening that's their affair.

> "you may dispute a line. disputing a line means producing the hour it happened in. I don't take testimony, I take *hours*. bring me the hour and I'll strike the line."

that sentence is the whole game. say it clearly.

### the contract

a real object. let them handle it. three feet of birch-paper, and:

- five names as parties of the second part, in five different hands, all of them theirs
- one signature on the line marked **for the second part, undersigned**
- the signature is legible and none of them recognize the hand
- where the amount should be, a blank

the signature can be matched. **DC 20 Investigation** comparing it to anything, or — much better — against **the guest book** in the wreckage (§12), whose front page has been torn out. the crew page. whoever tore it out was destroying a handwriting sample and doing a bad job of it at five in the morning. comparing what's left narrows the signature **to two PCs** and no further. land this in Act III and let them stew.

### the ledger

print this. put it on the table. cross lines off with a real pen.

| # | as it is written | what it means | how it settles |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | *one (1) crossing, Secomber-side, held nine hours — charged to the hosts* | the gate, billed to the crew | see 6. only one of these two can stand. |
| **2** | *catering, ad libitum, the whole of the oat field* | Secomber's harvest, eaten and danced flat | the **2 a.m.** Scrap. settle by restitution (Winnow can put oats back if she's asked properly), by squaring it with Reeve Cull, or with the Bill's Standing. |
| **3** | *one (1) name, surrendered freely, no duress* | a PC's name is in somebody's pocket right now | the **1 a.m.** Scrap. settle by getting it back, or by that PC deciding out loud to leave it. |
| **4** | *labour: B. Orrick, in perpetuity* | the fiddler | the **5 a.m.** Scrap. cannot be struck. can be **rewritten**. §10. |
| **5** | *one (1) Encore, conjured, unresolved* | the thing under the tarp | resolved, not killed. §9. |
| **6** | *tolls: five (5) memories, one per crossing — charged to the guests* | what they paid walking in | see 1. |
| **7** | *the balance* | blank amount, real signature | §10.5. |

**lines 1 and 6 are the same crossing billed twice** — once as hosts, once as guests. the party was crew. crew are not guests. **DC 15 Investigation** to notice the double-bill, and then one clear sentence to Quietus strikes whichever line they disprove. she is not embarrassed. she strikes it and writes *contested, allowed* in the margin and moves on. the **9 p.m.** Scrap proves which they were, if they want to be certain.

hand out Standings. cut to Act II.

## 5. scraps and going back

this is the load-bearing mechanic. read it twice before you run it.

a **Scrap** is one recovered hour of last night. getting a Scrap earns the right to **go Back** — the table stops being dawn and becomes that hour, and the players play it out. nobody at this table listens to a monologue about what their character did. they do it.

### how a Scrap is earned

three ways. only three.

1. **a witness gives it.** satisfy what they want (§6). this is most of them.
2. **an object triggers it.** something from the pockets table or the wreckage table, held by someone who was there. the object is the door; you still have to walk through it.
3. **Puck sells it.** he has the receipts, he sells them one at a time, and the price is always a favor payable *now*. §7.

### framing a Scrap

before any dice, the DM says three things out loud:

- **the hour.** a specific time. write it on the table. the night gets built backwards in front of them and that alone is worth the price of admission.
- **the question.** one sentence. what this hour answers.
- **the cut.** the exact condition that ends the scene.

announcing the cut up front is the trick that makes the whole mechanic work. players who know how their scene ends will steer toward the ending. players who don't will circle for twenty minutes. good cuts:

> *when someone says the word "fine." / when the music changes. / when the first blow lands. / when anyone walks out of the firelight. / after four exchanges, whatever's happening. / the moment somebody gets what they came in for.*

then hand it over and shut up. a Scrap runs **5–10 minutes of real time.** six Scraps is roughly fifty minutes of the session, which is exactly the budget.

### who plays whom

**default: everyone plays themselves, last night.** same character, no memory of a future, three drinks into the best air in the multiverse. crucially: **the player establishes what they were doing.** they narrate first. the DM only contradicts when a witness's account already says otherwise.

**a PC who wasn't in that hour doesn't sit out.** hand that player an index card with a name, one **want**, and one **secret**, and they play someone in the crowd. everyone plays every scene. nobody watches. write six of these cards before session and reuse them; the players won't notice and the scenes get better.

### what carries forward

- **no present-day resources are spent Back.** not slots, not HP, not uses. cast whatever you want. it was last night; it's already paid for.
- **no damage carries forward either.** if a PC gets hurt in a Scrap, they don't lose HP — instead the DM names a mark that is *already on their body* this morning. a burn on the wrist. a missing eyebrow. a bite. it explains something and it costs nothing.
- **the Take-Away.** every PC who participated in a completed Scrap takes exactly one of these, DM's choice, or the player's if they argue well:
  - **an object.** you find it on you now. you had it all along.
  - **a fact.** a name you can use, a lie you can now tell truthfully, a door you now know about.
  - **Heroic Inspiration**, if the scene got somewhere real.
  - one per PC per Scrap. it does not stack and it does not bank across Scraps.
- **relationships stick.** a Scrap that establishes a real connection with an NPC grants **Advantage on the next social check with that NPC**, once. mark it on the sheet.

### how a Scrap goes wrong

two ways, and both are good for the session.

**the Contradiction.** if the players establish something that flatly contradicts an account already on the table, the Scrap **spoils.** it still happened — you can't unhappen an hour — but the ledger records the discrepancy and the party gets nothing from it. **advance the Fold by 1.** Quietus loves a discrepancy. she'll say so.

**the Overreach.** a player who reaches for something too convenient — *"obviously I took the contract off him and burned it"* — makes a **Memory Check: d20 + Charisma modifier, no proficiency bonus.** it isn't a skill. it's how good you are at believing yourself.

- **DC 13. Success:** it's true, and it always was.
- **Failure:** the **opposite** is true, always was, and the DM narrates it with a completely straight face.

this is the funniest rule in the document. use it constantly. never warn anyone it's coming.

### two hard rules

1. **no hour twice.** once an hour is played, it's fixed. nobody goes back to fix a Scrap.
2. **the night comes out backwards.** the available Scraps run from dawn toward dusk — 5 a.m., then 3 a.m., then 2 a.m., then 1 a.m., then 9 p.m. **except the last twenty minutes of it**, which assembles when it's ready and not before. see 5.6.

### 5.1 THE TARP — 5 a.m.

**triggering fragment:** the torn grommet from the pockets table, Bell Orrick's account, or Puck's cheapest receipt.
**the question:** how do you put a thing like that to sleep?
**the cut:** when the canvas settles.

six of them dragging a hayrick tarp across a shape the size of a cart while Bell plays something slow and does not stop and will not look at anyone. someone lays a line of salt. someone argues that salt is for a different kind of problem. somebody is holding a grommet so hard it comes off in their hand.

**what it reveals:** the tarp is not a lid, it's a **promise** — the thing under it agreed to lie down *for now*, on the understanding that it gets the rest of the song later. Bell has been paying the interest on that promise ever since, one note at a time. and in the corner of the scene, if anyone looks: a PC coming back from the direction of the gate at a fast walk, with ink on their hand.

**settles:** ledger line 4.

---

### 5.2 THE ENCORE — 3 a.m.

**triggering fragment:** Bell Orrick, if they've been relieved. Winnow, if she's been apologized to. nobody sells this one.
**the question:** whose idea was it?
**the cut:** the first note.

Hew Marrin has been on his feet since nine. seventy-eight years old, Secomber's own, the man who taught Bell to hold a bow, and he has not sat down once. at twenty to four he stops in the middle of a reel and goes down, and six hundred people who are entirely out of their minds on Feywild air do not understand what they have just watched, and they start chanting for one more.

let the players be in that crowd. let them chant. **do not tell them what they're doing.**

something answers. it comes up out of the pit — stage lumber and borrowed coats and every voice in the field — and it plays, and it is, at the time, the funniest thing that has ever happened.

**what it reveals:** the tune. its name and how it goes. whoever holds this Scrap can name it in Act IV, which drops the Performance DC from 20-with-Disadvantage to 15 flat. it also reveals that nobody called the Encore deliberately. six hundred people asked for one more song and something in that field was listening.

**settles:** nothing on its own. makes line 5 solvable.

---

### 5.3 THE FIELD — 2 a.m.

**triggering fragment:** Puck's receipt, or Reeve Cull describing what she saw from the road.
**the question:** who agreed to feed six hundred fae, and with what?
**the cut:** when someone says the word "fine."

a negotiation, at two in the morning, conducted at volume, in a field, by people who have been working for five hours and drinking for four. on one side: a delegation of hungry fae with excellent manners. on the other: a member of the party, holding a clipboard they do not remember acquiring, with the authority to say yes.

play the fae delegation absolutely straight. they are courteous, reasonable, and they will accept the phrase *"help yourselves"* as a binding conveyance of an entire crop.

**what it reveals:** which PC said it. do not decide beforehand — let the scene find out. this is the comedy Scrap; let it run long if the table's enjoying it.

**settles:** ledger line 2, provided somebody then does something about the oats.

---

### 5.4 THE TRADE — 1 a.m.

**triggering fragment:** the ring, the tooth, or Nimble Grist, who will lie about it beautifully.
**the question:** what did you give away, and to whom?
**the cut:** when hands come apart.

behind the drink table, out of the light. two figures making a swap. somebody's name goes across the table wrapped in a cloth, and something else comes back the other way. it's a fair trade. everyone in the scene thinks so.

**run this one dirty:** before the scene, quietly check whether anybody at the table has noticed that **one PC has not been addressed by name since they woke up.** nobody will have. NPCs have been calling them *you*, *friend*, *the tall one*. the moment somebody clocks it, that's the trigger, and the Scrap becomes about them.

**what it reveals:** the name is currently in the pocket of a fae who is asleep in a boot approximately forty feet from where the party is standing (wreckage 7). getting it back is a conversation, not a fight, and the fae is furious and hungover and will drive a hard bargain from inside the boot.

**settles:** ledger line 3.

---

### 5.5 THE GATE — 9 p.m.

**triggering fragment:** free from Puck, who wants them to have this one. also available from Winnow.
**the question:** what did you pay to get in?
**the cut:** when the last of them steps over the line.

nine at night, the ring not yet cut, the crop still standing. five crew arriving to work a job, and a small formality at the boundary. each of them puts something down and does not pick it up again.

**hand out index cards.** each player writes one memory their character held dear — a sentence is plenty. collect them face-down. that's the hole. that's what's been missing all morning, and they now know its shape.

**what it reveals:** they crossed as **crew**, on Puck's hire, not as guests. that kills one of the two crossing charges outright.

**the cards come back:** all five, at noon, if the party settles five or more ledger lines. each returned memory is **Heroic Inspiration** and thirty seconds of table silence while its player reads it aloud. that is a real ending beat and it costs you nothing but the discipline to shut up during it.

**settles:** ledger line 1 or 6, party's choice.

---

### 5.6 THE LAST SCRAP — 4 a.m. *(gated)*

**do not run this before the Encore is down.** Puck will not sell it, the witnesses genuinely do not have it, and Winnow was not there. it surfaces when the field goes quiet and not one second earlier.

**the question:** why did you sign it?
**the cut:** when the pen leaves the paper.

four in the morning. the Encore is playing something enormous in the pit. four men are carrying Hew Marrin out toward the road, and every single person they pass assumes he's just drunk, and two of them cheer. Bell Orrick is standing on the boards playing along with the thing, crying, and has not missed a note.

Puck is at the edge of the light with three feet of birch-paper and an expression that is, for once, not amused.

> "it doesn't hold. what they've built out there doesn't hold — it comes apart at dawn and it takes the field with it, and the town after that, because that is what happens to a thing that was never finished. somebody has to stand for it."
>
> "not for long. just until somebody ends it properly."
>
> "and I'm not putting my name to a number I can't see."

run this scene for **the Signatory alone**, at the table, out loud, with everyone listening. it is not a private scene. it is the best thing that happens all session and everyone should get to watch it.

**what it reveals:** they didn't sign to save the field. they looked at Bell Orrick playing alongside a thing that sounded like their teacher, and at four men carrying that teacher out past a crowd that was still cheering, and they bought Bell one more hour. the amount is blank because the price is *whatever it costs*, and the Signatory knew that, and signed anyway, and then walked back and tore a page out of the guest book so nobody could match the hand.

**give the Signatory's player the final word on why.** ask them: *"you'd been awake for eleven hours and you'd known Bell for nine. why?"* whatever they say is canon. do not improve on it.

**settles:** ledger line 7 — but only if the Encore has been **resolved** rather than killed. see §10.

## 6. act II: the survivors

this is the body of the one-shot. seventy-five minutes of talking to people about a party.

every witness has a **want**, a **price**, and a **hard line**. none of them can be beaten purely with dice. all of them can be beaten with attention.

---

### WINNOW, FIRST FAIRY
*sober, appalled, awake for thirty-one hours*

she told them. she told all of them, repeatedly, in writing, and she is not going to raise her voice about it now because raising your voice is what **they** did.

- **wants:** an apology. specifically. from someone who means it and who names the actual thing they did.
- **will trade:** everything. she was sober all night, she remembers the entire thing in order, and she will hand it over completely and immediately to anyone who takes her seriously.
- **how to get it:** no roll. there is no Persuasion DC on Winnow and you should say so if a player reaches for the dice. **she cannot be lied to** — not "high Insight," she simply knows, no roll, tell them. she can be *apologized* to, and it has to be about something real.
- **hard line:** she will not discuss Bell Orrick with anyone who thinks it's funny.

> "there were nine of us running that field and six hundred of you enjoying it, and I want you to understand that I am not angry. I have gone past angry. I am at the part where I write it all down."

she holds the **9 p.m.** and **3 a.m.** Scraps. she can also put the oats back — an hour's work and a genuine request, and Secomber's harvest stands up again. she will not offer. somebody has to ask.

---

### PUCK
*promoter, ringmaster, holder of the receipts*

he is having a wonderful morning. he is standing on a hay wain in yesterday's coat with a cup of something and he is *delighted* to see them.

- **wants:** entertainment, and to not be the one holding the paper.
- **will trade:** four of the six Scraps, individually, priced (§7).
- **how to get it:** he doesn't need convincing, he needs paying. lying to him is **DC 21** and he will tell you the number before you roll.
- **hard line:** he will not sell the **3 a.m.** or the **4 a.m.**, and if pressed he says why exactly once:

> "I don't sell things I was wrong about."

that is the only sincere sentence Puck speaks all session. deliver it flat, move on immediately, and do not let anybody follow up.

**running him honest:** Puck will tell them what happened, who did it, and what it cost. he will leave himself out of the sentence every single time. he isn't sorry. he is, however, genuinely fond of these five, which is worse.

---

### BELL ORRICK
*mortal fiddler, twenty-three, nine hours in, not going home*

sitting on the edge of the boards, playing. has been playing since nine o'clock last night. cannot stop — not "won't," **cannot**, and has three levels of Exhaustion to show for it (§11).

- **wants:** to stop. that's it. that's the whole want.
- **will trade:** the truth about the Encore, the tune, and the shape of the promise under the tarp.
- **how to get it:** relieve them. any PC who takes over the playing for one full round buys Bell the ability to speak in complete sentences. **DC 12 Performance** to hold it, or automatic for the Backline. this is the single most important small mercy in the session and the party will almost certainly stumble into it.
- **hard line:** they will not leave the ring and will not discuss why.

play them **cheerful.** that's the whole trick. Bell is not tragic at anyone. they will make the party laugh twice before the party works out what has actually happened here, and then it lands like a dropped anvil.

> "no, it's fine — genuinely, it's fine, I'm the best I've ever been. nine hours. you know what I'd give for nine straight hours of practice? well." *(they don't stop playing.)* "well, apparently that."

---

### REEVE DAMSON CULL
*Secomber's reeve, standing at the edge of her own field since first light*

nine townsfolk behind her. she cannot cross the ring and has been trying, on and off, for two hours, in a way that has gone from furious to methodical.

- **wants:** her harvest. failing that, somebody's neck, and she isn't picky.
- **will trade:** the only outside view of the night that exists. she watched the whole thing from the road. she saw who went in, who came out, and what the light did at three in the morning.
- **how to get it:** she is hostile and she is **correct**, which is the difficulty. **DC 22 Persuasion** to convince her the party isn't to blame — and they shouldn't make it, because they are, partly. the real solution is to stop arguing and offer something: the oats back (Winnow), payment, or Hew Marrin's body handled properly. any of those and she talks freely, at length, and is unexpectedly good company.
- **hard line:** Hew Marrin was her uncle's oldest friend. if the party is flippant about him she is done, permanently, and the 2 a.m. Scrap gets much harder to reach.

> "I have stood here since it was dark enough to need a lamp and I have watched my crop being *enjoyed*. so let me be very plain with you. I do not care what you remember."

---

### NIMBLE GRIST
*a hob who was on the bill and got cut*

small, aggrieved, immaculately dressed, has not slept and doesn't intend to.

- **wants:** credit. he wants, more than anything anyone else in this field wants anything, to have played.
- **will trade:** a great deal of information, all of it false.
- **how to get it:** **DC 17 Insight** to catch him lying, which is nearly hopeless. **DC 15 Insight after his second lie** to catch the *pattern* instead: every lie Grist tells is an exact inversion of something he genuinely saw. invert him and he becomes the most reliable witness in the field. tell the players when they crack it. let them enjoy it.
- **hard line:** none. he has no hard lines. that's the problem.

**the trap and the tool:** Grist will happily claim credit for anything if you let him, and **the ledger records claims** (§11). the party can hand him a line item and he will take it, gladly, publicly, because it means he was *involved*. he does not understand what he is taking. whether they let him is a real decision and you should not signpost it either way.

### the sleepers

seven people asleep in the oats. waking one takes **DC 13 Medicine** or a bucket. each gives one detail that is useless right up until it isn't. roll on **d6: what a sleeper remembers** (§12), reroll duplicates, and if you have the time in a five-hour session, wake all seven.

## 7. act III: the field

sixty minutes of walking the wreckage, buying receipts, and circling the thing under the tarp without lifting it.

### the wreckage

roll or pick from **d10: wreckage of the field** (§12). three or four items is plenty. two of them matter enormously:

- **the guest book** (9) — rain-swollen, six hundred names, front page torn out. that page had the crew's signatures on it. comparing what's left against the contract narrows the signature to **two PCs**. name those two out loud and then change the subject.
- **the tarp** (10) — do not lift it yet. every instinct the party has will be to lift it. reward the delay: anything they learn before the fight makes the fight winnable without a fight.

### Puck's market

he sells receipts one at a time and prices them personally.

| receipt | the hour | Puck's price |
|---|---|---|
| **the tarp** | 5 a.m. | tell Winnow, out loud, in front of everyone, that the festival was a good idea. |
| **the field** | 2 a.m. | he borrows one PC's face for ten minutes. he will use it. you will hear about it later. |
| **the trade** | 1 a.m. | give him something you can't get back. he'll tell you honestly whether it counts. |
| **the gate** | 9 p.m. | free. he wants them to have this one, and he will not explain why. |

he will not sell 3 a.m. or 4 a.m. see §6.

**if the party tries to take the receipts:** they find lint. they will always find lint. he does not fight over it and he is not offended; he thinks less of them for the attempt, which is worse and which he will mention once, later, at a bad moment.

### the tarp

forty feet of grey hayrick canvas over something the size of a cart, weighted at the corners with stage lumber, ringed by an unbroken line of salt somebody laid at four in the morning with a very steady hand.

it is breathing. not fast. about once every forty seconds.

**DC 15 Stealth** to get a look under it without waking it. success buys one clear observation — it is made of coats, and lumber, and something else — and the fight starts on the party's terms. failure starts Act IV immediately, which is also fine.

**it wakes anyway** when any of the following happens, and you should let the party do all three:

- the Fold reaches 6
- anyone plays music within thirty feet
- anyone shouts

## 8. optional: the counter-claim

**thread this through Acts II and III if you need the extra hour.**

Nimble Grist files a counter-claim with Quietus. he asserts that he was on the bill, that he played, that the festival's success is substantially attributable to his set, and that he is therefore owed. none of it is true. all of it is filed correctly.

Quietus is obliged to process it. processing it means finding **one witness** who will say, on the record, that they heard Nimble Grist play last night.

**there isn't one, because he didn't.** but there are five people standing in this field who cannot remember the night at all, and any one of them could say it and be believed.

- **if a PC lies for him:** the claim lands. Grist becomes a party to the contract — which splits the debt six ways instead of five, materially improves the ending, and saddles a small hob with a share of something he cannot possibly pay. he is *thrilled*. he thanks them. he does not stop thanking them.
- **if nobody lies for him:** the claim is dismissed at 11 a.m., in front of everyone, in four words, and he sits down in the mud and does not get up.

there is no correct answer here and you should not supply one. either way it's **+1 line settled** on the ledger and a thing the table will still be arguing about in the car park.

## 9. act IV: the encore

**trigger:** the tarp comes off, one way or another.

> the canvas slides and keeps sliding, and the thing under it stands up in sections.
>
> it is made of the stage. it is made of forty borrowed coats and a drum kit and the boards off the front of the hay wain, and it is fourteen feet of it, and where a face would be there are six hundred open mouths in the shape of one open mouth.
>
> it does not look at anybody. it turns its head toward the loudest thing in the field.
>
> and it waits, with enormous patience, for someone to give it the rest of the song.

### what it is

at twenty to four in the morning, six hundred people asked for one more song, and something in that field was listening and did not know how to refuse. the Encore is not Hew Marrin. it is not a ghost and it is not a haunting. it is the shape of six hundred people wanting a thing that had just stopped being possible, and it has been holding a note for two hours waiting for a coda that nobody has written.

it does not know it kills people. it has not noticed yet.

### encounter build

- 1 × **the Encore** — CR 5, **1,800 XP**
- up to 3 × **Sleepwalker** — CR 1/4, 50 XP each, **150 XP**
- **total: 1,950 XP**

against the 2024 DMG budget for five level-3 characters (**Low 1,375 / Moderate 2,750 / High 4,250**), this lands at **142% of Low and 71% of Moderate.**

**why there:** the Encore is a solo, and a solo's XP always overstates its threat against five PCs who each get a turn. budgeting above Low is not generosity, it's correction. the Sleepwalkers barely add damage — what they add is **turns the party spends not attacking the boss**, because every one of them is a sleeping person being worn, and every one that gets pulled up is 10 Temporary Hit Points on the thing they're trying to bring down. that's the pressure. it isn't the HP.

**designed for 3–4 rounds.** 105 HP plus up to 30 temporary, against five level-3 characters putting out something like 35–50 a round, is three rounds of focused fire and four if they're splitting attention. they will split attention. that's the design.

**scale up (hard table):** start two Sleepwalkers already up (+100 XP, 2,050 total), give the Encore 20 more Hit Points, and let *Feedback* recharge on 4–6.
**scale up (much harder):** add **Legendary Actions, 2/round** — *Stomp* (one *Downbeat*) and *Roar* (each creature within 10 feet makes a DC 16 Constitution save or has the Deafened condition until the end of its next turn). worth roughly +1 CR.
**scale down (short on time, or a light party):** no Sleepwalkers, *Feedback* once per fight, and Bell Orrick starts already relieved so the Performance out is live from round one.

### ending it without killing it

**this is the real solution and it is the reason the whole investigation exists.**

the Encore was summoned mid-tune and never got an ending. give it one.

- **three successful DC 15 Charisma (Performance) checks, on three different rounds**, by any creature that can be heard, playing **the tune**.
- **naming the tune requires the 3 a.m. Scrap** (5.2) or Bell Orrick's help. without either, the party is guessing: **DC 20 with Disadvantage**, and they'll know they're guessing.
- **Bell can carry it** — a PC who has relieved Bell may substitute Bell's Performance modifier for their own. Bell is at three levels of Exhaustion and it is still the best fiddling anybody in this field will ever hear.
- **each success** also means the Encore does not use *Pull One Up* on its next turn.
- **a failure by 5 or more** means the Encore uses *Feedback* on its next turn if available. wrong notes hurt.

on the third success it sets down whatever it's holding, sits, and comes apart into coats. take a beat. let the field be quiet. **then run 5.6.**

if they kill it instead, that also works, and the ledger records line 5 as *unresolved, written off*, and somebody eats the interest. don't punish the choice. just let the ending be a colder one.

### terrain

the pit is a knee-deep trench of mud, dropped boots, and sleeping people: **difficult terrain** within the Encore's 20-foot Emanation. the boards of the stage are 4 feet up and clear ground, and they are also the loudest place in the field to stand. two braziers are still lit at the corners. the seam of the Fold runs across the eastern third of the field and it is **moving**, about a foot a minute, and anything on the wrong side of it at noon goes with it.

## 10. act V: noon

the Encore is down. the field is quiet in a way it has not been since yesterday evening. there is maybe half an hour left, and the seam is now visibly crossing the ground where the crowd stood.

### 10.1 the Last Scrap

run **5.6**. everything in the session has been paying for this.

### 10.2 the accounting

Quietus comes back along the seam with the ledger open. she hears them out, line by line, in order, and she strikes what they've earned. she does not gloat and she does not stall. if they've been clever she says so, once, in a tone that could be a compliment.

line 7 — **the balance** — is the last one, and it can only be settled by the Signatory saying, out loud, in front of everyone, what they did and why. not a check. not a roll. a sentence.

> "the amount is blank," Quietus says, "because you signed for a thing that hadn't finished happening. it's finished now." *(she looks at the field, at the coats, at the girl in the mud with the fiddle.)* "so it comes to exactly what it came to."

### 10.3 Bell Orrick

line 4 cannot be struck. Bell made the deal, Bell meant it, and Bell is not leaving the ring — not because anyone is cruel but because that is where they were standing when they said yes.

what the party can change is **what kind of never.**

- **line 4 unsettled:** Bell goes with the fae half at noon, still playing, still unable to stop, and the last thing anybody sees is their hands.
- **line 4 settled:** Bell can stop. they put the fiddle down. they stretch, and it takes a while. and then they pick it back up, because they want to, and they walk across the seam of their own accord playing something they *chose*, and they wave.

that is the whole difference and it is worth the entire session.

### 10.4 the fold

at noon the far half of the field rolls up. give it a full round of warning — the seam is visible, the light changes, the rooster stops — and then a **DC 15 Dexterity save** for anyone caught on the wrong side. failure means they go with it. that is not death and it is not permanent, but they are not at breakfast tomorrow and somebody will have to go and get them.

then it's a trampled oat field outside Secomber at midday with the sun out, nine townsfolk on the road, and six hundred pairs of abandoned shoes.

### 10.5 endings by lines settled

| lines | ending |
|---|---|
| **0–2** | *the collection.* Quietus does what the ledger says. she takes the Signatory — not their life, their **next seven years**, served in the Marches, adjusting other people's mornings. she is not unkind about it. the party can come and get them. that's a campaign. |
| **3–4** | *the arrangement.* the debt is restructured rather than discharged. one season of service, owed jointly, callable at any time, and Quietus will absolutely call it. the party walks out of the field free and scheduled. memory cards stay unpaid. |
| **5–6** | *the quietus.* the ledger closes. Quietus writes *settled* across the bottom, hands the contract to whoever is closest, and says the nicest thing she is capable of saying, which is *"that was a clean circuit."* the memory cards come back. read them aloud. |
| **7** | *the receipt.* everything above, and then she turns the page and refunds the toll with interest — the party gets **the whole night back at once**, all nine hours, standing in the mud at noon. it is far too much. two of them sit down. one of them laughs and cannot stop. every PC now remembers, in complete detail, exactly what they were like at three in the morning, forever. |

**modifier:** if the Encore was killed rather than resolved, every ending above gets one degree colder, and the coats stay on the ground, and nobody in Secomber can quite explain why the field is a bad place to stand after dark.

**last beat:** Puck is on the hay wain with the same cup, in the same coat, in broad daylight, looking like exactly what he is. he raises it to them. he says the only thing there is to say about last night, which is that they should have seen it.

When it drops to 0 Hit Points it falls over and the person inside is Unconscious but stable, whatever the damage type, whatever the intent. **It cannot be killed.** Tell the players the moment the first one goes down — before that, let them sweat.

**ACTIONS**

***Flailing Dance.*** *Melee Attack Roll:* +4, reach 5 ft. *Hit:* 5 (1d6 + 2) Bludgeoning damage.

### DM one-page recap
1. **dawn:** pockets roll, Quietus reads the contract, the Ledger goes on the table, the Fold goes on the table. *"bring me the hour and I'll strike the line."*
2. **the mechanic:** hour, question, cut — say all three, then hand it over. players play themselves; everyone else takes a crowd card. no resources spent Back. Take-Away is one object, one fact, or Heroic Inspiration. Contradiction spoils and advances the Fold. Overreach is DC 13 flat and the failure is funnier than the success.
3. **witnesses:** Winnow is free to anyone sincere. Puck sells four hours and won't sell two. Bell has to be relieved before they can talk. Damson Cull is hostile and correct. Grist is inverted truth and a live liability.
4. **the field:** the guest book narrows the signature to two PCs. the tarp is a promise, not a lid.
5. **the Encore:** 1,950 XP, 3–4 rounds, it attacks the loudest thing, area damage hits the sleepers, three DC 15 Performance checks on three rounds ends it without killing it — and naming the tune needs the 3 a.m. Scrap.
6. **the Last Scrap:** run it out loud for the Signatory with everyone watching. give them the final word on why. do not improve on their answer.
7. **noon:** strike the lines, settle the balance with a sentence, let Bell choose the tune, save vs. the seam, count the ledger, read the memory cards.
8. **Puck on the hay wain**, in daylight, raising the cup. they should have seen it.
