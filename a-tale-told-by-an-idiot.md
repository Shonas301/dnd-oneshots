# A TALE TOLD BY AN IDIOT
### a midsummer one-shot for D&D 2024 (5.5e)

**party:** 5 PCs, level 3, all fae or fae-touched
**runtime:** 3–5 hours
**weight:** heavy roleplay, one short combat, zero dungeon
**setting:** Mullenwake, a cider-and-wool village in Deepingdale, on Midsummer night

---

## 1. at a glance

some years ago Puck spent a bored season tormenting a shoeshine boy named Bill in a world of coal-smoke and playhouses. the boy took it badly, then took it well, then wrote it all down. what he wrote was so *flattering*, so full of moonlight and lovers and a Queen radiant in her folly, that Titania has not shut up about it since.

she has decided this deserves a sequel.

Titania has summoned every subject of the Summer Court to a **renewal of vows** between herself and Oberon, staged in a mortal village yanked a half-step into the Feywild for the night. she has hired the party as **staff**. she has privately set Puck loose to ruin it.

**the secret:** both monarchs want a *story*, not a ceremony. a flawless wedding is a fine story. a catastrophic wedding is a *better* story. the only failure state is being boring.

**the players will not know this** until the end. they'll believe they're choosing between doing their jobs and burning it all down. let them agonize. it's funnier.

### session flow

| act | content | time |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | the Hawthorn Gate: arrival, the Toll, assignments | 30 min |
| **I.5** | Puck's offer: each PC recruited privately | 20 min |
| **II** | three Preparations: Feast, Play, Crown | 90 min |
| **III** | the Uninvited, the one combat | 40 min |
| **IV** | the vows: resolution and reveal | 40 min |

**trim for 3 hours:** cut one Preparation (the Play survives cutting best).
**expand to 5:** run the changeling subplot (§7) and let the Preparations sprawl.

### safety
charm, memory theft, and identity-loss are load-bearing here. do a two-minute session zero: lines and veils, an X-card or equivalent, and an explicit "charm effects never override your character's dignity — you stay in the driver's seat, you just drive somewhere stupid."

---

## 2. the ballad clock

this is the spine of the one-shot. put it on the table where everyone can see it.

> **THE BALLAD CLOCK — 10 VERSES**
> somewhere in this village, someone is writing all of this down.

add a **Verse** whenever something happens that a bard would actually bother to sing about: a grand gesture, a spectacular disaster, a genuinely clever lie, a moment of real feeling, a goat in a wedding dress. loyalists and saboteurs both add Verses. so do the NPCs.

**do not tell the players what the clock measures.** let them theorize. loyalists will assume it's a ruin-meter and panic when it fills. saboteurs will assume it's a win-meter and panic when it *doesn't*. both wrong. both entertaining.

**rough pacing:** 1–2 Verses per Preparation, 2 for the combat, 2–3 in Act IV. aim to land at 7–9 by the vows.

**boredom rule:** if 20 minutes pass without a Verse, Puck personally intervenes to create one, and it will be at the party's expense.

---

## 3. the player characters

everyone is fae or fae-touched, and everyone is **staff**. that's the load-bearing bit. a split party stays in the same rooms, every PC has a reason to be in every scene, and "working class heroes" goes literal.

### suggested species (2024 PHB + expansions)
Goblin (Fey), Changeling (Fey), Harengon, Fairy, Satyr, Eladrin, Gnome, Centaur, Hexblood, or any species with the **Fey-Touched** origin feat and a good story.

### the five posts

| post | job | class fits |
|---|---|---|
| **the Under-Cook** | Peaseblossom's kitchen. knows what's in the punch. | Druid (Land), Ranger, anyone with Cook's Utensils |
| **the Fiddler** | the processional music. everyone must dance. | Bard (Glamour or Lore) |
| **the Doorwarden** | guest list, coats, throwing people out. | Fighter, Barbarian (Wild Heart), Paladin (Ancients) |
| **the Seamstress** | Titania's train. Oberon's collar. sixty yards of moth-silk. | Rogue (Arcane Trickster), Wizard (Illusionist) |
| **the Hedge-Warden** | tends the hawthorn ring. knows how the gate works. | Warlock (Archfey), Ranger (Fey Wanderer), Druid |

each post grants a **Perquisite**, a once-per-session narrative permission, not a spell:

- **Under-Cook:** you may declare that one dish or drink is already in your hands, and say what's in it.
- **Fiddler:** you may declare that a piece of music is playing right now, and name it.
- **Doorwarden:** you may declare that one creature is on, or not on, the guest list. the list agrees with you.
- **Seamstress:** you may declare that you have already altered one garment a creature is wearing.
- **Hedge-Warden:** you may open a shortcut through the hedge between any two places in the village.

these are *outrageous* and that's correct. hand them out and watch what happens.

## 4. act I: the hawthorn gate

**open here. skip the travel.**

> the village of Mullenwake smells of cut hay and cider apples, and it has not smelled of anything else for four hundred years. tonight it smells of frost and roses, which is wrong, because it is Midsummer.
>
> a hedge of hawthorn has grown around the village square since sundown. it is nine feet tall. it is in bloom. through the gap in it you can see the well, the mill, and a great many lanterns that no one in Mullenwake owns.
>
> a small woman in a coat of woven grass is checking names off a list of unrolled birch bark. she does not look up.
>
> "staff or guest?"

### the toll

**Thistlewick, First Fairy** (§10) is Mistress of Ceremony and the party's direct supervisor. she is kind and exhausted and organized to the point of illness. she will process them in under a minute.

then comes the gate. Titania's law: *nothing mortal passes into the Court without paying something mortal.*

**mechanic, the Toll.** hand each player an index card. they write down **one memory their character holds dear**. a sentence is plenty. collect the cards face-down. that memory is *gone*. the character knows only that there is a hole where something used to be.

> "it doesn't hurt," Thistlewick says, not unkindly. "it's like a tooth. you'll keep finding the gap with your tongue all night."

the cards matter later. two ways they come back:

1. **Puck offers one back** as the price of cooperation (§5). he'll read it aloud. it will be the most personal thing said all evening.
2. **Titania returns them all** at the vows, but only *if* the Ballad Clock is at 5+. each returned memory grants **Heroic Inspiration** and a quiet moment at the table. give each player thirty seconds to narrate remembering.

**DM mood dial: aware or tricked?** split the difference. Thistlewick tells them exactly what the job is. nobody tells them what the job is *for*.

### the briefing

Thistlewick walks and talks. three things must be ready by moonhigh:

1. **the Feast.** Peaseblossom is three ingredients short and one nervous breakdown deep.
2. **the Play.** the mortals of Mullenwake are performing an entertainment. it is not going well. it has never gone well.
3. **the Crown.** Oberon's gift to Titania is a circlet of the first light of the first morning. it is not finished. Oberon is not present. Oberon has, in fact, gone missing.

> "anything you cannot fix, you hide. anything you cannot hide, you make deliberate. do you understand? nothing here is a mistake. everything here is *on purpose*. that is the entire job."

hand out the Perquisites. cut to Act I.5.

## 5. act I.5: puck's offer

**run this as a series of 90-second private scenes.** pull each player aside, or just narrate it at the table while everyone else listens. Puck doesn't care who hears, and the dramatic irony is worth more than the secrecy.

Puck approaches each PC **individually**, in a form tailored to them: as a fellow servant, as a dog, as their own reflection in the punch bowl, as the person whose memory they just surrendered.

his pitch, adapted per PC:

> "she's got you polishing spoons. *spoons.* four hundred years she's been married to that man and the only night anyone remembers is the one where it all went wrong. do you know why? because it *went wrong.*
>
> i'm not asking you to hurt anyone. i'm asking you to help me make this the best night in the history of the Court. same as her. different method."

**the bribe:** he produces the PC's stolen memory card and reads a line of it aloud. he'll return it for a single favor: something small and specific and deniable. examples:

- swap the salt and the sugar. that's all. just that.
- tell Peaseblossom that Cobweb said the thing about her mother.
- leave the west gate unlatched for six minutes at moonrise.
- put this frog in that boot. don't ask.
- let Dob Cranny think his performance is good.

**expect a split party. that is the design.** two loyalists, two saboteurs, and one person playing both sides is the ideal configuration. no PvP is required. sabotage and repair happen in the same rooms, at cross purposes, in real time. encourage opposed checks rather than combat between PCs.

**if the whole party refuses Puck:** he shrugs, pockets the memories, and becomes a *complication generator* instead of an ally. fine. run the Preparations as pure crisis management with Puck as the recurring hazard.

**if the whole party joins Puck:** Thistlewick becomes the obstacle, and she is far more competent than she appears. also fine. run the Preparations as heists.

## 6. act II: three preparations

each Preparation is a **scene with two win conditions**. loyalists want the Fix. saboteurs want the Ruin. both add Verses. give each roughly 30 minutes, and let players walk away from any of them unfinished. an unfinished Preparation is *also* a story.

### 6.1 the feast
**location:** the converted mill. steam, shouting, forty-one courses.
**NPC:** **Peaseblossom** (§10). head cook. magnificent. three seconds from tears at all times.

the problem: three components are missing.

| component | where it is | Fix | Ruin |
|---|---|---|---|
| **honey from a hive that has never seen winter** | in the hedge. the bees are Cobweb's and Cobweb is sulking. | DC 13 Persuasion on Cobweb, or DC 15 Nature to harvest without her | tell the bees Titania called them common |
| **a pear stolen from a mortal who deserved it** | Old Mother Vance's tree. she does not deserve it. | DC 15 Investigation to find *someone* who deserves it, or a genuinely clever moral argument | steal from someone who plainly doesn't. the pear turns bitter and the whole table tastes it |
| **the last word of a dying man** | nobody in Mullenwake is dying. | roleplay. the village grave-digger's father died last winter and he remembers. DC 13 Insight to earn it gently | Puck suggests solving the shortage the obvious way. do not let this land, but let them *feel* it land |

**set piece:** the punch. six gallons of it. whoever controls the punch controls the wedding. if it's tampered with, roll on the hijinks table (§11) for what happens to the first twenty guests who drink it, and add a Verse.

### 6.2 the play
**location:** the barn.
**NPC:** **Dob Cranny**, wheelwright and self-appointed director. uses *Commoner* with Charisma 16 and no self-awareness whatsoever.

nine mortals of Mullenwake are staging *The Most Lamentable Tragedy of the Moon and the Miller's Daughter*. they believe they are doing this voluntarily. they are not sure why. they have been rehearsing since dawn and cannot stop.

**the truth:** Titania geased the entire village into performing, then forgot to specify a *good* play, and Dob Cranny volunteered. the compulsion ends at moonhigh regardless.

**comedy engine:** Dob wants notes. he wants notes desperately. he will ask every PC for notes and accept none of them. he has cast himself as the Moon, the Miller's Daughter, and the Wall.

- **Fix (make it good):** DC 15 Performance to coach the cast, DC 15 Persuasion to convince Dob to cut his own monologue, or any PC simply takes a role and carries it. success = the mortals are accidentally moving, and Titania weeps. **+2 Verses.**
- **Ruin (make it worse):** trivially easy. the challenge is making it worse in a way that's *interesting*. require a plan, not a roll. best outcome: the play accidentally reenacts a real scandal of the Court. **+2 Verses.**
- **the trap:** if a PC breaks the geas early (DC 17 Arcana, or removing the hawthorn stake under the barn floor), nine mortals wake up mid-performance in front of the entire Summer Court. that's a Verse, but Thistlewick will never forgive them.

### 6.3 the crown (and the missing king)
**location:** the well, then the hedge.
**NPCs:** **Moth** and **Mustardseed** (§10), who were supposed to be finishing the circlet and have instead been arguing for six hours.

the circlet needs **the first light of the first morning**, which is stored in the bottom of the Mullenwake well and has been since before the village existed. retrieving it requires someone to go down and *not look at it*. DC 15 Wisdom save, or be blinded for 1 hour and spend the rest of the night describing what they saw. (blindness is a small price. what they saw is a Verse.)

**the real scene:** Oberon is missing.

have a PC find him. he is **not** in crisis. he has **not** been kidnapped. he's sitting on the churchyard wall in a farmer's coat, watching the mortals rehearse the play, drinking cider from a chipped cup. he'd like a moment. he'd like someone to talk to who isn't a courtier.

this is the emotional center of the one-shot. play him tired and enormously charming. he'll ask the PC:

> "four hundred years. do you know what the trouble is with being loved by someone who cannot be surprised? — no, don't answer that. nobody ever answers that."

he knows about Puck. he *approved* Puck. he will not say so. but a PC who rolls **DC 18 Insight** (or asks a sharp enough question) catches it: *he is not worried about tonight at all.* foreshadow, don't reveal.

he'll come back when he's ready. he is never late. he simply arrives when the story needs him, which is a different thing.

**+1 Verse** for the conversation. **+1 more** if a PC says something true to him.

---

## 7. optional subplot: the changeling

thread this through the Preparations if you need the extra hour.

eight months ago the Court took a Mullenwake infant and left one of their own in the cradle. the mortal child, now called **Nettle**, is a page at the wedding and perfectly happy. the changeling in the cradle, called **Tam** by his mortal parents, is also perfectly happy.

nobody is being harmed. both mothers are content. undoing it would devastate four people.

there is no correct answer. do not provide one. if the party solves it, that's **+2 Verses** regardless of which way they solve it. if they refuse to touch it, that's fine too, but have Nettle wave at them during the ceremony.

---

## 8. act III: the uninvited

**trigger:** moonhigh, immediately before the vows. the hawthorn hedge freezes solid.

> the lanterns do not go out. they ice over, which is worse.
>
> through the gate comes a wedding procession. a bride in a gown of frost-eaten lace, and four attendants who were, until quite recently, guests at this wedding. they are all still smiling. none of them are moving their smiles.
>
> the bride does not look at Titania. she looks at Oberon, and she begins to speak, and the words are the words of the vow.

**who:** an emissary of the **Queen of Air and Darkness**, sent to speak the vow *first*. fae law is a matter of who says the words. if she finishes, she is wed to Oberon, and the Gloaming Court gains a claim on the Summer Court's heart.

**why both factions fight:** Puck's mayhem is *love*. this is not. Puck will fight beside the party and say something unusually sincere about it. this is where saboteurs learn the difference between ruining a wedding and ending one.

### encounter build
- 1 × **Bride of the Long Winter** (CR 4, 1,100 XP)
- 4 × **Rimeling Attendant** (CR 1/4, 50 XP each = 200 XP)
- **total: 1,300 XP.** by the 2024 DMG budget for five level-3 PCs (Low 1,375 / Moderate 2,750 / High 4,250) this sits just under Low, and that is deliberate. the pressure is the vow clock, not the damage. designed for 3–4 rounds. if your table eats it, add two Rimelings and start the Bride on line two.

### the clock: the vow of winter
the Bride speaks **one line of the vow at the start of each of her turns** (no action required). she needs **four lines**.

- if she starts her turn **Incapacitated, Silenced, Stunned, or Frightened**, or grappled with her mouth covered, she loses that line.
- if she takes **20+ damage in a single round**, she loses a line already spoken.
- if she speaks the fourth line: Oberon is bound, Titania's face changes, and the one-shot pivots. see "if the vow completes" below.

**announce the lines aloud.** one through four. the players will feel every one.

### non-violent outs (reward them)
- **DC 18 Persuasion / Insight.** she is not here willingly. she was a Summer courtier once. someone who knew her name can break her.
- **the Doorwarden's Perquisite.** declaring her *not on the list* ends the effect outright and is a legendary use of a Perquisite. **+2 Verses.** let it work.
- **the Under-Cook's Perquisite.** a toast she accepts makes her a guest. guests cannot marry the host.

### terrain
the square is iced: difficult terrain, and any creature that ends its turn prone there takes 1d4 cold. two braziers remain lit. a creature within 5 feet of one is immune to the Bride's *Killing Frost* aura. five PCs, two safe spots. let them fight over it.

### if the vow completes
don't fudge it. Oberon goes still and cold, Titania stands, and the temperature in the room drops out of the *fiction* and into the *table*. then Puck steps forward and says he'll go in Oberon's place, because the vow named a *king*, and Puck has been king of exactly one thing for exactly one night, and he can prove it.

he can't. it's a lie. it's the best lie he has ever told, and fae law is made of lies well told. **+3 Verses**, Puck leaves with the Bride, and the ending becomes bittersweet and enormous. this is the better ending. do not warn them.

---

## 9. act IV: the vows

the Court assembles. the play is performed, well or badly. the feast is served, safe or otherwise. Oberon takes his place. Titania takes hers.

and Titania stops the ceremony, and calls the staff forward by name.

### the reveal
in the back of the crowd, polishing a boot he isn't looking at, is a mortal boy of about fifteen with ink on his fingers and a notebook he has been filling all night. he does not belong here. he belongs to a world of coal-smoke and playhouses, and he has no idea how he got here, and he has not stopped writing since he arrived.

> "you did wonderfully," Titania says, to whichever of them tried hardest to fix it. "and you," she says, to whichever tried hardest to ruin it, "did wonderfully."
>
> "there was never a wrong answer. there was only a dull one." she looks back at the boy. "he gave me a play once. i thought i should give him another."

return the memory cards, if the Clock is at 5+. let the players read their own aloud.

then the vows, actually spoken and actually meant. they are short and plain and not funny at all. earn this by playing everything before it as broadly as you like.

### endings by verse count

| verses | outcome |
|---|---|
| **0–3** | *the quiet wedding.* it goes off. nobody sings about it. Titania is gracious and disappointed, and the boy's notebook stays half-empty. memories are not returned. the Toll is kept as payment for a wasted evening. |
| **4–6** | *a good night.* memories returned. the party are named Friends of the Court: one favor each, redeemable in any future game. |
| **7–9** | *the second play.* memories returned with interest. each PC also recovers something they'd forgotten they'd forgotten. the boy writes it all down. in four hundred years there will be a play about tonight, and every PC is in it, and none of them are flattered by the portrayal. |
| **10** | *the ballad of Mullenwake.* everything above, plus Mullenwake never quite comes all the way back out of the Feywild. it's still there. the cider is extraordinary. the party can always find the road to it, no matter where they are. |

**last beat:** whatever happened, Puck is sitting on the hedge as they leave, and he tips his cap, and he says the thing he always says.

**Languages** Common, Elvish, Sylvan; telepathy 120 ft.

***Magic Resistance.*** Puck has Advantage on saving throws against spells and other magical effects.

***Never Truly Caught.*** If Puck would be reduced to 0 Hit Points, he instead drops to 1 Hit Point, laughs, and teleports up to 1 mile away. He does not return during the current scene.

***Shifting Shape.*** As a Bonus Action, Puck assumes the appearance of any Small or Medium creature or a Tiny beast. His statistics are unchanged. DC 22 Insight or truesight to see through it.

**ACTIONS**

***Multiattack.*** Puck makes three attacks, in any combination of *Puckish Touch* and *Cutting Word*.

***Puckish Touch.*** *Melee Attack Roll:* +9, reach 5 ft. *Hit:* 14 (2d8 + 5) Psychic damage, and the target has the Charmed condition until the end of Puck's next turn.

***Cutting Word.*** *Ranged Attack Roll:* +10, range 60 ft. *Hit:* 13 (2d6 + 6) Psychic damage, and the target has Disadvantage on the next attack roll it makes before the start of Puck's next turn.

***Love-in-Idleness (Recharge 5–6).*** Puck anoints the eyes of one creature he can see within 60 ft. *Wisdom Saving Throw:* DC 18. *Failure:* The target has the Charmed condition for 1 hour, and perceives the first creature it sees after this effect as its beloved. It will not willingly harm that creature. *Success:* No effect. The target repeats the save if it takes damage from the "beloved."

***An Ass's Head (1/Day).*** *Charisma Saving Throw:* DC 18, one creature within 30 ft. *Failure:* The target's head becomes that of a donkey for 1 hour. It cannot speak intelligibly, has Disadvantage on Charisma checks other than Performance, and has Advantage on Performance checks, because it is *hilarious*.

**BONUS ACTIONS**

***Merry Wanderer.*** Puck teleports up to 60 feet to an unoccupied space he can see.

**REACTIONS**

***Mistaken Identity.*** When Puck is hit by an attack, he swaps places with a willing or Charmed creature within 30 feet. That creature is hit instead.

**running Puck:** he never lies about *what* he wants, only about *why*. he keeps every promise in the least useful way possible. he is not cruel. he is a comedian with unlimited budget and no editor. if a player out-tricks him, he is delighted, and says so, and immediately does something worse.

### THISTLEWICK, FIRST FAIRY
*Small Fey, Lawful Neutral* | Mistress of Ceremony
