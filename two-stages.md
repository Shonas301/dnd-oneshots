# TWO STAGES
### a one-shot for D&D 2024 (5.5e)

**party:** 5 PCs, level 3, all fae or fae-touched
**runtime:** 3–5 hours
**weight:** heavy roleplay, one short combat, one very loud tug-of-war
**setting:** the Sough, a valley off the High Moor, between the villages of Hurst and Skellow, on a summer night

---

## 1. at a glance

every hundred years the Sough is claimed. the law is old and stupid and nobody has ever bothered to change it: at moonhigh, whichever fire in the valley has the most mortals standing around it holds the Sough for a century. this is called **the Count**.

Titania read the law the way you read a contract you intend to win. she booked the valley floor, put up a main stage, and assembled a bill that has been in planning for eleven years.

her sister read it the way you read a contract you intend to *break*. the Queen of Air and Darkness has a side stage half a mile downhill, unsanctioned, no permits, no bill posted, and a sound rig that can be felt in the teeth from Hurst.

the mortals of Hurst and Skellow walked four miles for the noise. they think it's a fair. they have no idea they are being counted.

**the job:** there is one crew in the Sough tonight and there are two stages. both stage managers signed the same five names, and both of them knew. the party works both. everyone does. that's the job.

**the secret nobody at either stage will say out loud:** the mortals get a vote, and it's cast with their feet, and neither court has ever once considered that they might walk somewhere else.

### session flow

| act | content | time |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | load-in: the Sough, the two bosses, five posts | 30 min |
| **I.5** | two offers, one hour: both courts buy the party in the open | 25 min |
| **II** | working the bill: the Draw, the Plays, Nightjar's set | 100 min |
| **III** | the fence line: the shove, and then the knife | 40 min |
| **IV** | moonhigh: the Count, and the trees | 35 min |

**trim to 3 hours:** run the two offers as one table scene instead of five private ones. cap Act II at 65 minutes by allowing each PC one Play instead of two. cut Verenestra's set entirely — she's better as a rumor. the fence line does not shrink. do not shrink it.

**expand to 5 hours:** run §7 (Anwen Tace) as a full thread through Act II, let each Play be its own scene with NPCs in it, and let the party actually stand and watch Nightjar's set for real minutes of table time. that last one is worth more than it looks.

### safety

three things need a two-minute session zero.

**charm.** it's everywhere tonight. establish the line: a Charmed PC stays in the driver's seat and drives somewhere embarrassing. nobody narrates your character's interior for you.

**mortals as currency.** the entire premise is two immortal aristocrats treating four hundred farmers as a number on a board. that discomfort is the point, and it should stay a discomfort the *players* feel about the fae, not a thing the table does to the villagers for laughs. the mortals are people. play them as people. Anwen Tace in §7 exists specifically to make that non-negotiable.

**PvP-adjacent play.** the party will be bought by both courts and there is a stretch of Act III where PCs may shove each other. state up front: PCs can work against each other all night, and the one fight in this one-shot is explicitly nonlethal until it isn't, and when it turns, every PC is on the same side automatically. no PC ever rolls damage against another PC in this adventure. lines and veils, X-card or equivalent, the usual.

## 2. the draw

this is the spine. it lives on the table, in front of everyone, all night.

> **THE DRAW: 24 TOKENS**
> ten at the main stage. ten at the side stage. four already in the trees.
> one token is about ten mortals. there are no more coming.

three bowls, three chalked boxes, three strips of tape. use anything, as long as the players can see it from where they sit and reach it without asking. label the third one **THE TREES** and refuse to explain it.

unlike most clocks: **this one is completely public.** the players see it, the courts see it, and both queens can read it from where they're standing. there is no hidden information in the Draw. all the tension is in what it costs to move it.

### the rules

1. **every Play is declared.** before you roll, say which stage you're pulling for. out loud, in the fiction, in front of whoever is standing there. there is no such thing as a quiet Play.
2. **success takes tokens from the rival stage.** never from the trees. you can steal a crowd from a competitor. you cannot get it back from the woods.
3. **failure feeds the trees.** people who are being fought over notice, and wander.
4. **Drift.** at the end of every scene, move **1 token from the stage that is currently ahead** into the trees. tied? the stage that was loudest that scene.
5. **attention costs.** in any scene where the gap between the stages changes by 3 or more, move **1 extra token** into the trees.
6. **the trees never give back.** twice all night they do, and both times it has to be earned. see §6 (booking the fire) and §8.4 (Cobweb and Moth).

### what a check earns

most Plays are the **Influence** action or a tool check. some are just a good idea, and those don't need a roll at all — say yes and move two tokens.

| result | swing |
|---|---|
| beat the DC by 10+ | **+3**, and the rival's next NPC set moves nothing |
| beat the DC by 5–9 | **+3** |
| meet the DC | **+2** |
| miss by 1–4 | **0**, and 1 token to the trees |
| miss by 5+ | **2 tokens to the rival stage** |
| natural 1 | 2 tokens to the trees, and the story of what you did gets around both sites before the next scene |

**budget:** each PC gets **2 Plays across the night**. one PC may Help on another's Play (Advantage), once per scene. spending a post's Perquisite (§3) on a Play is an automatic **+3 with no roll** and burns the Perquisite.

### what each court does when it's losing

check the gap at the end of each scene and escalate. these are not optional colour — they are the pacing.

| gap | main stage behind | side stage behind |
|---|---|---|
| **2** | Ferrule moves an act up the running order and doesn't tell the act | Grist turns the sound up. it is already too loud. |
| **3** | Titania appears at the front of house and is *radiant* at people. +1 to the main stage, free. | the Gloaming bar goes free. +1 to the side stage, free. and 1 extra to the trees, because free drink makes people ambitious about walking. |
| **4** | Ferrule offers the party the Standing Pass again, doubled, in front of witnesses | Nightjar goes on early (§6.4). automatic **+2 side stage**, no roll, no counter. |
| **5** | Verenestra is told she is going on now and she says no, and then she says yes, and it is ugly backstage | Grist sends four Crew-Hands to the fence to "check the line" |
| **6** | **the fence line (§8).** | **the fence line (§8).** |

if the gap has never hit 6 by the two-thirds mark of your session, run §8 anyway. Grist has been waiting all night for an excuse and does not require a good one.

### how the trees drain both

Drift alone takes ten tokens out of the game over a normal night. attention costs take two or three more. run it honestly and the trees end up holding more than both stages combined, which means the Count fails, which means the ending in §9.

**this is the design. do not fudge it toward a court.** the party can absolutely beat it — suppressing Drift is what half the Plays in §6 are for — but they have to notice the problem first, and nobody at either stage is going to point it out, because nobody at either stage has looked at the trees since dusk.

## 3. the crew

everyone is fae or fae-touched, and everyone is **crew**. two stages, one crew, five posts. every post works both sites, which means every PC has a reason to be in every scene and a legitimate excuse to be standing anywhere in the valley holding anything.

### suggested species (2024 PHB + expansions)
Fairy, Satyr, Eladrin, Changeling, Harengon, Goblin, Gnome, Hexblood, Centaur, or anything with the **Fey-Touched** origin feat and a story about a grandmother.

### the five posts

| post | job | class fits |
|---|---|---|
| **the Runner** | messages, gear, and gossip between the two sites. the only person welcome at both fences. | Rogue, Monk, Ranger, anyone fast |
| **the Rigger** | light, rope, cable, stakes, decking. knows what's load-bearing. | Artificer, Rogue (Arcane Trickster), Wizard, Fighter with tools |
| **the Caller** | front of house at both stages. the between-set patter. the voice the crowd hears. | Bard, Sorcerer, Warlock (Archfey) |
| **the Bar** | the punch at both bars. six hundred cups. one ladle. | Druid, Ranger, Cleric, anyone with Cook's or Brewer's kit |
| **the Ground** | walks the crowd. minds the mortals. finds the lost ones. | Paladin, Cleric, Barbarian, Fighter, anyone with Insight |

### perquisites

each post grants one **once-per-session narrative permission**. not a spell. not a check. a declaration.

- **the Runner — *the Message.*** declare that you already delivered a message tonight. name who got it and what it said. they received it, they believed it, and it came from whoever you say it came from.
- **the Rigger — *the Cut.*** name one thing on either site that is rigged, lit, tied, or standing. it isn't. it comes down at the moment you choose, and it looks like nobody's fault.
- **the Caller — *the Announcement.*** say one thing over the crowd. every mortal in the Sough hears it, believes it for ten minutes, and acts on it.
- **the Bar — *the Round.*** a round has already gone out. name who got it and what was in it. they drank it.
- **the Ground — *the Sweep.*** name a mortal, or a hundred of them, and say where they are standing right now. you put them there an hour ago and nobody remembers you doing it.

spend one on a Play and it's an automatic **+3**. spend one on a scene and it will probably break the scene, which is fine, that's what they're for. hand them out written on cards at the end of Act I and then stop talking about them.

## 4. act I: load-in

**open in the middle of the load-in.** nobody travels to a festival in a one-shot.

> the Sough is a green cut in the High Moor about a mile long, and by afternoon there are two stages in it.
>
> the main stage is at the top, hung with forty years of banners that were made for tonight. it faces downhill. it is beautiful. it took nine days.
>
> the side stage is at the bottom, half a mile away, and it is four wagons, a scaffold, and a wall of horn-cones the size of a barn. it took an afternoon. it is already making a noise. it has been making that noise since noon and nobody has gone on yet.
>
> between them, running the whole half mile, is a rope on ash stakes at about waist height. that's the Line. both courts agreed to it before dusk, in front of witnesses, and the agreement is short: **while the Line stands, nothing at the fence sheds blood.**
>
> a satyr with a slate clipboard is walking uphill toward you at speed. behind him, a long way behind him, something enormous in a canvas coat is walking downhill toward you at the same speed, and neither of them has looked at the other once.

### both bosses, at once

this is the tone-setter. run it fast and let them overlap.

**Ferrule** (§10) is the main stage's manager. six hundred years of bills. he talks in running order. he is not warm but he is scrupulously fair, and he will tell the party the truth about everything except how worried he is.

**Grist** (§10) is the side stage's manager. he is not fae nobility, he is not clever, and he is the best in the valley at putting a heavy thing where it needs to go. he was hired eleven days ago. he is trying very hard.

they both hired the same five people. neither will acknowledge this. both will pretend the other's contract is the irregular one.

> "you're on my sheet," says Ferrule, not looking up.
>
> "they're on mine," says Grist, from thirty feet away.
>
> "yes," says Ferrule. "that's how it works. that's how it's always worked. there's one crew." he turns a page. "there is a *reason* there's one crew and if either of you ever finds out what it is, don't tell me."

### the briefing

Ferrule walks and talks. Grist walks on the other side of the Line and talks over him. give the players both briefings simultaneously and let them pick sentences out of the noise.

**Ferrule wants:** the banners hung by first dark, the bar staffed, the running order held to the minute, and Verenestra kept happy, which is a full-time post he does not have the budget for.

**Grist wants:** the horn-cones aimed, the bar loud, the running order ignored, and somebody to tell him honestly whether the main stage is better, because he cannot tell and it is eating him.

both mention the Count. neither explains it properly. if a PC asks directly:

> "at moonhigh they count heads," Ferrule says. "most heads holds the valley a hundred years. that's the whole of it. it's a bad law and it's the law."
>
> "do the mortals know?"
>
> Ferrule turns a page. "no. and if you tell them, they'll behave differently, and then it isn't a count of anything, is it."

hand out the posts. hand out the Perquisite cards. put the Draw on the table with its starting ten / ten / four and say nothing about the third bowl.

cut to Act I.5.

## 5. act I.5: two offers, one hour

**run as five private 90-second scenes, or run it at the table and let everyone listen.** the dramatic irony is worth more than the secrecy, and both courts already assume the party talks.

within one hour of dusk, every PC is offered a token by each side. this is not a trap and it is not a test. it is standard. both courts expect the party to take both. so does everyone else on the crew.

### the Standing Pass — Summer

a sprig of hawthorn on a green cord. worn at the neck, visible, warm. Ferrule hands them out like a man distributing tax forms.

> "that's a Standing Pass. it means I vouch for you. it means when this is over your name goes on the sheet, and the next sheet, and every sheet, and you will never again have to explain to anybody who you are before you're allowed to work."
>
> he finally looks up. "I have been on the sheet for six hundred years. it is the only thing I own."

**what Summer wants:** to be *watched*. not obeyed, not loved. watched. Titania has spent eleven years on a bill nobody at the side stage will ever hear about, and the terror underneath the whole main stage operation is that it might turn out not to matter.

**spending it (once):** you gain Heroic Inspiration, four Summer Crew-Hands do exactly what you say for one scene without question or report, and you may re-roll one Play.

### the Chit — the Gloaming

a slate disc on a black cord, cold, with a number scratched into it. Grist doesn't make a speech. he hands it over and says the number.

> "eleven. that's you. anything you want tonight, you say eleven and it happens and nobody asks anything. food, bodies, a wagon, a lie, somebody moved out of your way." he shrugs. "that's it. that's the offer. it isn't friendship. I'm not selling you friendship, I'd be bad at it."

**what the Gloaming wants:** to win *dirty*, publicly, and to have Summer know it. the Queen of Air and Darkness has not booked a single act with the intention of it being good. every slot on her board is a weapon aimed uphill.

**spending it (once):** you gain Heroic Inspiration, one specific thing you ask for happens with no questions, and you may turn one failed Play into a bare success — but the swing is halved (round down) and 1 token goes to the trees.

### wearing both

everyone wears both. Ferrule wears a Chit. Grist has a Standing Pass in his coat pocket and it is eleven days old and unworn and he thinks nobody knows.

**wearing is nothing. spending is what counts.** when a PC spends one token, the other court learns of it before the end of the next scene. always. that isn't a check, it's the weather.

### exposure

each PC tracks **Exposure**, 0–3, separately with each court. it is not a punishment meter. it's an attention meter.

| trigger | Exposure |
|---|---|
| you made a Play against a court while wearing its token | **+1** |
| you spent that court's rival's token | **+1** |
| you were seen doing something you couldn't explain | **+2** |
| you did that court a real, visible favour with nothing in it for you | **−1** |

after any Play made against a court, a PC may attempt a **DC 13 Deception, Stealth, Sleight of Hand, or Performance** check to keep it off the boards. success: no Exposure. failure: +1 anyway. **declining to roll is a legitimate move.** some PCs will want to be seen doing it. those PCs are correct and should be rewarded.

### the confrontation (Exposure 3)

that court's patron pulls the PC aside. roll or choose.

| d6 | what happens |
|---|---|
| 1 | they take the token back and say nothing else, ever. this is the worst one. |
| 2 | they double the offer, loudly, in front of the other court's people. |
| 3 | they ask you to prove it. one Play, right now, for them, in the open, with the other court watching. |
| 4 | they tell the rival everything. Exposure resets to 0 with both courts, and neither will leave you alone for the rest of the night. |
| 5 | they don't confront you. they confront a mortal you've been kind to. |
| 6 | a shrug. "everyone works both. I just like to know who I'm working with." Exposure drops to 1 and you have a genuine ally. |

### what betrayal actually costs

**there is no violence in this.** the fae expect double-dealing. what they do not forgive is being boring about it.

- **Summer's cost:** Ferrule takes your name off the sheet. not this sheet. every sheet. you'll work again — there's always work — but you'll never be listed, and nobody will ever know you were there.
- **the Gloaming's cost:** Grist pays you in full, immediately, exactly as agreed, and then tells everyone precisely what you were paid and what for. and then he never asks you for anything again.

say this to the players in the fiction, early, through Ferrule:

> "the cost of crossing us isn't a knife. don't be stupid. the cost is we stop bidding."

### the archfey offers

each queen makes exactly one approach, to one PC, at a moment of the DM's choosing during Act II. pick the two players who are enjoying themselves most.

**Titania**, at the front of house, during someone else's set, without warning:

> "you're the one who fixed the thing with the cups." she is not asking. "I want to give you something and I've been told I'm to ask first, which is new, and I don't like it. so: may I?"
>
> whatever the answer, she says: "you'll never be tired again. that's the whole gift. I'm told mortals find that one boring and I have never understood why."
>
> what she wants is one sentence, honestly given, about whether the bill is good. she will know if it's a lie and she will accept it anyway and that is much worse.

**the Queen of Air and Darkness** does not make an offer. she waits until a PC is alone, and asks a question, and leaves before the answer.

> the sound from the side stage doesn't stop, but you stop being able to hear it.
>
> "how many of them do you think came for the music?"
>
> she isn't looking at you. she's looking downhill at four hundred people with mud on their shoes.
>
> "I ask everyone. nobody has ever guessed low enough."

## 6. act II: working the bill

run Act II as short alternating scenes. **never let the party stand at one stage for more than ten minutes of table time.** the Runner exists to drag scenes across the valley; use them.

### 6.1 the bill — main stage

| slot | act | the DM's note |
|---|---|---|
| dusk | **the Hurst Morris** | nine mortals, bells, absolutely giving it everything. the mortals adore it. the fae find it physically painful. Ferrule booked it as a courtesy and it is the single most popular thing on the main stage all night. |
| first dark | **the Nine Cups** | satyr drumline. competent, loud, forgettable. |
| moonrise | **Bramblegrace** | a dryad choir. forty voices, one body, four hundred years of practice. it is genuinely sublime and everyone talks straight through it. |
| third hour | **the Sundering Wheel** | one gnome, one hurdy-gurdy, one temper. will stop mid-song to address hecklers by name. |
| moonhigh | **VERENESTRA** | the headliner. will not go on early. will not go on late. if she goes on at all, **+4 main stage**, and by then it does not matter. |

### 6.2 the bill — side stage

| slot | act | the DM's note |
|---|---|---|
| dusk | *(nothing)* | the stage is dark and the sound is already running. that's the joke. it works. |
| first dark | **Rooks' Parliament** | six masked fae, no instruments, all rhythm, entirely on the offbeat. hypnotic. slightly wrong. |
| moonrise | **Tally and the Hollow Boys** | four things in coats. one of the coats is empty and keeps time anyway. |
| third hour | **NIGHTJAR** | booked deliberately opposite Bramblegrace. see §6.4. |
| moonhigh | *(a smudge on the board)* | there is a slot and there is no name in it. Grist doesn't know. the crew doesn't know. it's the Queen, and she hasn't decided, and she may not use it. |

**use the smudge.** if the party ever needs a lever, the empty moonhigh slot on the Gloaming board is the biggest one in the valley, and the Caller or the Runner could conceivably put a name in it.

### 6.3 the plays

the menu. these are what the party *does* all night. offer them, don't enforce them — a good idea that isn't on this list is worth +2 and no argument.

| # | the play | check | DC | swing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **poach an act** — talk a booked performer across the fence | Persuasion or Performance | 15 | **+3.** the losing stage now has a hole in the running order; its next NPC set moves nothing |
| 2 | **cut the cable** — kill the other stage's light or sound mid-set | Sleight of Hand, Thieves' Tools, or Athletics | 14 | **+2.** Exposure +1 unless covered |
| 3 | **make the punch famous** | Cook's Utensils, Nature, or Persuasion | 13 | **+2.** if you spike it, roll on the hijinks table (§11) |
| 4 | **start a rumour about the other bar** | Deception | 13 | **+2**, and **+1 more** if you can point at a mortal actually doing the thing you claimed |
| 5 | **steal the echo** — reshape the valley so your stage carries | Arcana or Performance | 15 | **+2**, and 1 extra token to the trees. loud carries a long way. |
| 6 | **save a set that's dying** | Performance or Insight | 14 | **+2**, and **no Drift this scene** |
| 7 | **build the better fire** | Survival | 12 | **+1**, and 1 extra token to the trees anyway. fire draws, and not always to you. |
| 8 | **find the lost child** — and then actually play the scene | Perception or Investigation, then roleplay | 13 | **+2**, **no Drift this scene**, and the Ground gains a real friend in the crowd who is worth more later than any of this |
| 9 | **make the walk unpleasant** — bog, briars, a rumour of a bull | Athletics, Stealth, or Nature | 14 | **+2**; on *any* failure, 2 tokens to the trees instead of the usual |
| 10 | **the free round** | none. costs the bar's stock and the Bar's post takes the blame. | — | **+2.** once per bar, per night. |
| 11 | **name the headliner** — tell the mortals who's actually on later | Persuasion | 16 | **+3** if it's true. if it's a lie: **+3** now, **−4** at the start of the next scene, in public. |
| 12 | **be genuinely good at something in front of people** | any skill or tool you're proficient in | 15 | **+2**, or **+3** if the player says what they actually did |
| 13 | **book the trees** | see below | — | see below |

**booking the trees.** at some point somebody will notice the third bowl filling up and ask what's down there. anyone can walk down and look — DC 5, nobody's hiding it. what's down there is §9.

a PC with the authority — the Caller's *Announcement*, the Runner's *Message*, or Ferrule's or Grist's signature — can put the man with the fiddle on a stage, in a slot, on the bill. if they do:

- move **half the tokens in the trees (round up)** to that stage. immediately. it is the single biggest swing available all night.
- and Ferrule will tell them the price out loud before they commit, because Ferrule is fair: **a mortal who is put on a fae bill is a fae asset.** he doesn't go home. everyone in the valley will have seen the transaction and no court will undo it.

let them do it if they want to. state the cost plainly, once, and then honour their decision without editorial.

### 6.4 nightjar's set

**the Gloaming act that is genuinely, unfairly better.** this is not a check the party can beat. it is a fact they have to react to.

third hour, booked opposite Bramblegrace. **Nightjar** (§10) walks out with no band and no light and sings for eleven minutes.

> she sings in a woman's voice, and it is not her voice, and it is not an illusion — you can hear the difference, all of you can, it's the one thing crew always hear. that voice belonged to somebody. it was sold, and it was paid for, and the price was fair.
>
> the main stage is running a forty-voice choir four hundred years in the rehearsing. by the third minute you can hear it losing.

**mechanically:** Nightjar's set is an automatic **+2 to the side stage** with no roll and no counter-Play. she does it again at any point the side stage falls 4 behind (§2). there is no Play on the menu that stops it.

**what the party can actually do:**
- **out-poach it.** get her onto the main stage. DC 20 Persuasion, and she'll say no, and she'll mean it, and the reason is §7.
- **give the voice back.** the only real answer. requires §7.
- **stand there and let it happen**, and admit to Ferrule afterward that the side stage has the better act, which is the single most useful true thing anyone says to him all night and moves nothing on the Draw and matters more than most of what does.

### 6.5 cobweb and moth

the emotional centre. seed it in Act I, feed it through Act II, resolve it in §8.4.

**Cobweb** rigs light for the main stage. **Moth** rigs light for the side stage. they built lights together for four hundred years and they have not spoken since the bill was posted.

what Moth will tell you, if asked: *Cobweb took the Summer booking.*

that is not it.

what actually happened: Cobweb took the Summer booking and didn't say, and Moth found out three days later from a Crew-Hand, in a queue, for a bar. four hundred years and Cobweb didn't say. Moth signed with the Gloaming that same afternoon, out of spite, and has regretted it every hour since, and would rather rig a stage she hates until the heat death of the Feywild than admit that out loud.

and Cobweb's side, which Cobweb will never volunteer: **it never occurred to Cobweb that it was a question.** Cobweb assumed Moth was coming. Cobweb has been standing on the main stage for nine days waiting for Moth to turn up and start work.

both are true. both are stupid. both of them know exactly how stupid. neither will go first.

**how to run them:** they will each talk to the party at length about everything except each other. they will each ask, very casually, whether the other stage's rig is any good. they will each accept a message from the Runner and neither will send one.

**getting them to say the other's name is DC 20** and should never be rolled — it isn't a check, it's the last beat of §8.4.

## 7. optional subplot: anwen tace

thread this through Act II if you want the fifth hour. it costs about forty minutes and it is the best forty minutes in the file.

**Anwen Tace** of Skellow is seventy-one years old and walked four miles tonight in boots she borrowed. she is somewhere in the crowd, and the Ground will find her, because the Ground always finds the one who's standing still.

forty-two years ago Anwen sold her singing voice to something at a crossroads. what she got for it was a winter — one specific winter, in which her sister did not die. she has never once regretted it and she will say so briskly, to anyone, without being asked twice.

she came tonight because a carter told her there was an act down the bottom end of the valley that sounded like a woman from Skellow.

**she does not want it back.** if Nightjar offers it — and Nightjar will, immediately, appallingly, in a rush, the second the two of them are in the same place — Anwen will decline before the sentence is finished. she is seventy-one. what would she do with it.

**what she wants** is smaller and it wrecks people. she wrote a second verse. she wrote it *after* she sold the voice, a long time after, and no one has ever heard it, because she has never been able to sing it.

if a PC gets these two in the same place — and it takes about nine minutes of honest work, no rolls, just a Runner who carries something true and a Ground who won't take no from either of them — Anwen teaches Nightjar the second verse, badly, in a flat croak, with a fae headliner listening like her life is in it.

**payoffs:**
- Nightjar will not play the side stage again. she is done for the night. **the side stage loses the automatic +2.**
- at moonhigh, Nightjar goes down to the trees, unbooked, unpaid, and sings the second verse to about two hundred farmers who have no idea what they are hearing. **move 4 tokens from each stage to the trees.**
- Heroic Inspiration to every PC who was in the room, and one of them should be allowed to cry about it.

**if you skip this subplot:** the third fire still works. Nightjar still walks down at the end, uninvited, and sings something. it lands about eighty percent as hard, which is plenty.

## 8. act III: the fence line

**trigger:** the gap on the Draw hits 6, or the two-thirds mark of your session time, whichever comes first.

> it starts the way these always start, which is with nothing. a Crew-Hand from one side leans on the Line to say something across it. a Crew-Hand from the other side leans back to answer. the rope takes the weight of both of them and holds, because it's a good rope, and that is the last thing that goes right for eleven minutes.
>
> by the time you get there, there are sixteen bodies at the fence and everybody is laughing and nobody has hit anybody yet, and half of them are people you were drinking with an hour ago, and both stages are still playing.

### 8.1 the shove

**before initiative: every PC declares a side, out loud.** they are crew. they have both tokens round their necks. this is the one moment in the night where they have to stand somewhere.

PCs may end up opposed. that's allowed here and only here, because nobody can get hurt while the Line stands.

**setup.** the Line runs across the field. mark its position; it starts at 0. roll Initiative normally, including passive scores. Grist and four Gloaming Crew-Hands on one side, four Summer Crew-Hands on the other, PCs where they said.

**damage becomes Ground.** while the Line stands, **no Hit Point damage is dealt by anything.** roll attacks and saves as normal. roll the damage as normal. then read it off this instead:

| damage rolled | effect |
|---|---|
| 1–9 | target is pushed **5 ft.** away from the Line |
| 10–19 | pushed **10 ft.** |
| 20+ | pushed **15 ft.** and has the **Prone** condition |

track each creature's total **Ground Lost** in feet. a creature at **30 feet of Ground Lost is out** — winded, sitting in the wet grass, laughing at the sky. it does not come back.

**conditions apply normally**, with one change: anything that would impose the **Unconscious** condition instead imposes **Prone** and **Incapacitated** until the end of the target's next turn.

the **Shove** option of the Unarmed Strike works as written and adds 5 ft. of Ground Lost on a success. spells work. everything works. it is enormously funny to watch a level-3 Wizard *Fire Bolt* someone thirty feet backwards without singeing them.

**moving the Line.** at the end of each round, count creatures within 10 feet of the Line on each side. the side with more moves the Line **5 feet** toward the other. tie: no movement.

**winning the shove.** if the Line ends a round 20 feet into one side's ground, that side has won, and it's over, and everyone gets up. **the winning stage takes +2 on the Draw. 2 tokens go to the trees regardless**, because a brawl at the fence is precisely the sort of thing four hundred farmers walk away from.

### 8.2 the knife

Grist cuts the Line at the first of these:

- the Line moves 10 feet into Gloaming ground
- the top of round 3
- any PC spends a Chit or a Standing Pass during the brawl

> Grist doesn't shout. that's the thing you'll remember. he steps back out of the press, takes something out of his coat, and cuts the rope, and the rope makes a noise going that you will hear for a century.
>
> the knife is iron. cold iron, at a festival, in front of two queens, and every fae in that crowd knows exactly what that means in the same half-second, and the laughing stops in a way that laughing is not supposed to be able to stop.
>
> "right," says Grist. "now it counts."

**when the Line parts:**

1. all damage is real from this point.
2. every creature takes **1d6 damage per full 10 feet of Ground Lost** (maximum 2d6). Bludgeoning unless something else obviously fits. this is the night catching up.
3. **every PC is now on the same side. say this out loud at the table.** whatever they declared in 8.1 is void. this is not a court thing anymore. this is a Grist thing.
4. Summer Crew-Hands still standing fight beside the party. Gloaming Crew-Hands still standing fight beside Grist.
5. Initiative order does not change.

### 8.3 the encounter

- 1 × **Grist, Gloaming Stage Manager** — CR 5, **1,800 XP**
- 4 × **Gloaming Crew-Hand** — CR 1/2, **100 XP each = 400 XP**
- **total: 2,200 XP**

2024 DMG budget for five level-3 characters: **Low 1,375 / Moderate 2,750 / High 4,250.** 2,200 sits at 80% of Moderate and 160% of Low.

**why it lands there.** Grist deals zero Hit Point damage for the first two rounds — during the shove he only pushes. his budget doesn't start cashing in until round 3. four Summer Crew-Hands fight alongside the party unbudgeted, and one to three Gloaming Crew-Hands are usually already out of the brawl by the time the rope parts. felt difficulty lands near **Low**, which is correct: the pressure in this fight is the *moment*, not the attrition. designed for **3–4 rounds total** — two of shove, two or three of fight.

**the one number to watch.** Grist averages 28 damage a round, 33 with *Call It*. that will drop a level-3 PC who eats a full round of it. he attacks whoever is nearest the Line, never the weakest thing in reach, and that is the tell that saves them. keep it true even when it's inconvenient.

**scale up:** add 2 Gloaming Crew-Hands (→ 2,400 XP). or add 1 **Gate-Hand** (CR 3, → 2,900 XP, over Moderate). or give Grist **Legendary Resistance (1/Day)** and let him cut the Line at the top of round 2.

**scale down:** cut 2 Crew-Hands (→ 2,000 XP). or have Grist enter at the top of round 3. or bring Ferrule in at round 3 instead of round 4.

### 8.4 getting out of it

killing Grist works and is the worst available option. all of these are better and all of them should be visibly on the table.

- **retie the Line.** two creatures, one holding each end, one action each, **DC 15 Dexterity (Sleight of Hand)** or any tool proficiency you can argue for. the Rigger can do it alone. success ends the fight immediately: the compact snaps shut and every Bloodied creature within 30 feet sits down whether it wants to or not.
- **the count.** any PC who points downhill and says the crowd has gone. Grist's crew are paid by the head and they can all do arithmetic. **DC 14 Persuasion**, or just a good enough line. they down tools. Grist keeps swinging alone, which is much sadder and much easier.
- **take the knife.** it's an object. **DC 15 Sleight of Hand**, or a grapple and an Athletics contest. without it Grist stops, because without it he's a man who cut a rope in front of two queens.
- **ask him why.** **DC 15 Insight**, or one honest question. he's been promised the Sough's crew contract for the next century if the side stage wins. nobody has ever given him anything. he will say this in about nine words and then he will be very hard to fight.

**afterward.** neither queen defends him. cold iron at a festival is the one thing Titania and her sister agree about without discussion, and they both look at the party, and Ferrule says, evenly:

> "he's yours. both courts will abide by whatever you say. don't rush it."

five stagehands have just been handed a judgement that two archfey declined to make. let that sit for as long as the table needs.

### 8.5 the resolution: the same rope

run this in the quiet directly after the fence, while both stages are limping on emergency rigging and there is nobody left to watch either of them.

**preconditions.** the party must have done two things during the night, deliberately or by accident:

1. **carried something true between them.** any PC honestly relayed something one of them said to the other, without improving it. an improved version doesn't count, and the DM says nothing about that at the time.
2. **given them a reason that isn't the party's.** a job neither can do alone. the Line coming down does this by itself. so does the Rigger's *Cut*, if it takes both rigs at once.

if both are met, run the scene. **no rolls. do not allow a roll.**

if they aren't met, the party gets one more window at moonhigh: Cobweb and Moth both end up at the trees, on opposite sides of the same fire, and somebody has to speak first, and the party can make sure somebody does.

**the scene.** the party's whole job is to make sure neither of them has an exit. put them in the same place, hand them both an end of the same cable, and then walk away, which is the hard part, and which the table will want to fight you on.

> for a while neither of them says anything and both of them work, because the work is there and they're good at it, and it takes two.
>
> then Moth says, to the cable: "you didn't tell me."
>
> and Cobweb says, "I didn't know I had to," which is the worst thing Cobweb could have said and also exactly true, and Moth starts to laugh, and it's awful, and then it isn't.

**payoff.** ***Two Hands on the Same Rope*** fires for the first time in a year and a day. every light in the Sough comes on at once — both stages, the fence, the trees, the bar, four hundred lanterns nobody remembered hanging — and for about four seconds the whole valley is lit, and every mortal in it stops walking and looks up.

**mechanically, the party chooses one:**
- **move 3 tokens from the trees to either stage.** name it.
- **or move 3 tokens from each stage to the trees.**

this is one of the two times all night the trees give back. it's the party's call, made in the open, at the climax, and both queens are watching them make it.

## 9. act IV: moonhigh

the Count is at moonhigh and nobody administers it. it just happens, the way weather happens. both queens come up to the broken Line to be present for it, because it would be undignified not to, and they stand about ninety feet apart and do not look at each other.

read the Draw. that's the whole ceremony.

### the trees

whoever goes down there — and someone will — finds this:

> there is a fire in the trees, in a clearing that is not really a clearing, more a place where the bracken gave up. it is a bad fire, built by somebody who has built about four fires. it smokes.
>
> there is a man sitting on a log with a fiddle. he is twenty-two and his name is **Rab Ockley** and he is from Hurst and he knows three tunes and he is playing the second one, which he plays worse than the first.
>
> around him there are more people than are standing at either stage.
>
> there is nothing here. no magic, no working, no glamour, no compact, no bill. it is worse than both stages by every measurement you have been trained to make. it has more people at it.
>
> his cousin lit the fire because the queue at the top bar was long.

Rab will offer you a drink out of a jar. he will be embarrassed about the fiddle. he will ask, at some point, whether you're with the fair, and he'll mean it kindly, and it will be the only question anyone asks the party all night that has no angle in it.

**both queens walk down.** separately, at the same time, from opposite ends, which they will both later insist was a coincidence. neither of them goes into the firelight. they stand at the edge of it, in the dark, about forty feet apart, watching two hundred farmers listen to a bad fiddle.

and Titania turns to whichever PC is nearest and asks — quietly, and not rhetorically, and this is the last real beat of the one-shot:

> "what is he *doing*?"

she does not know. she has eleven years and forty banners and a choir with four centuries in it and she genuinely does not know. do not answer for the player. whatever they say is the ending.

### endings by the draw

| final state | ending |
|---|---|
| **main stage ahead, trees < both stages combined** | Summer holds the Sough a century. Titania's speech is exquisite and it is the fifth-best thing that happens tonight. what the party remembers is Ferrule's face when his name goes on a sheet that will outlive him. every Standing Pass is honoured permanently: the party never has to explain who they are to get work again, anywhere, ever. |
| **side stage ahead, trees < both stages combined** | the Gloaming holds it. no speech. the Queen of Air and Darkness looks at the number, nods once, and goes, and that's it, and the anticlimax is the point. every Chit is honoured for a century: name your number anywhere in the Sough and it happens. Grist, if he lives and if the party let him, gets his contract, and it is the only thing anyone has ever given him. |
| **dead tie** | no claim. deferred a hundred years. both courts have to come back and do all of this again and both of them look, briefly and identically, exhausted. the party is on both sheets for next time, whether they want to be or not. |
| **the trees hold as many as both stages combined, or more** | **the Count fails.** it does not fail on a technicality — it fails because the law measures fires and the biggest fire in the valley belongs to nobody. no court holds the Sough. no court can. the law was written by someone who assumed mortals were a quantity and it has just been broken by a shepherd who knows three tunes. |
| **the trees hold 16 or more** | all of the above, and the Sough is *mortal ground* now, permanently, in the way that some places just are. neither court can book it again. Hurst and Skellow will hold something in that valley every summer from now on and none of them will ever know why it feels like that, and in about ninety years it will be an enormous nuisance to the Gloaming Court, and Ferrule will find this very funny for the rest of his life. |
| **a stage won because the party booked the trees (§6.3)** | that court holds the Sough, and it is a clean, legitimate, well-earned win, and Rab Ockley does not go home. he's on the bill. he'll be on the bill for a long time. the party gets everything they were promised and Ferrule keeps his word to the letter, including the part he told them about in advance. |

### what each PC walks out with

- both tokens are honoured by whichever court won, regardless of what the PC did with them. neither court considers double-dealing a debt.
- any PC who ended the night at Exposure 3 with **both** courts is offered a permanent post by both, simultaneously, in the same conversation, and neither will let it drop.
- Cobweb and Moth, if §8.5 landed, are working the same rig next season and will deny that anything happened.
- Anwen Tace, if §7 ran, walks back to Skellow at dawn in borrowed boots and does not look back at any of it.

**last beat:** the fire in the trees burns down to nothing around three in the morning and everyone goes home, and there is nobody left in the Sough except the crew, taking down two stages, in the dark, together, because there is only one crew. end it there.

### DM one-page recap

1. **the Draw** goes on the table at the start of Act I: **10 main / 10 side / 4 trees.** every scene, Drift takes 1 from the leader into the trees. never explain the third bowl.
2. **load-in:** Ferrule and Grist both hired the same five people and both knew. hand out posts and Perquisite cards. mention the Count, don't explain it.
3. **two offers, one hour:** Standing Pass (Summer) and Chit (Gloaming), to every PC, openly. wearing both is normal. *spending* is what gets noticed. track Exposure 0–3 per court.
4. **Act II:** alternate stages every ten minutes. run the Plays menu (§6.3). Nightjar's set is an automatic +2 to the side stage that nothing on the menu can stop. seed Cobweb and Moth constantly.
5. **the fence line:** the shove is nonlethal — damage becomes **feet pushed**, 30 feet is out, the Line moves 5 ft. per round toward whoever has fewer bodies on it. Grist cuts the rope with cold iron at round 3. **every PC is on the same side after that. say it out loud.** Ground Lost converts to 1d6 per 10 feet. 3–4 rounds. four ways out that aren't killing him.
6. **the same rope:** put Cobweb and Moth on one cable and walk away. no rolls. the party then moves 3 tokens, either direction, in front of both queens.
7. **moonhigh:** read the Draw. that's the Count. then go down to the trees, find Rab Ockley and his three tunes, and let Titania ask the question she doesn't know the answer to.
8. **last beat:** two stages coming down in the dark, and one crew doing it. end there.
