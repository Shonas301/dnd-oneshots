# A TALE TOLD BY AN IDIOT: quickstart

### read this, start the game, read the rest during the first break

**you need:** five index cards, a pen, and the full doc open to §10 when the fight starts. nothing else.
**you are running:** a fae wedding where the PCs are the catering staff. heavy roleplay, one combat, 3–5 hours.
**the party:** 5 PCs, level 3, all fae or fae-touched, all **staff**.

---

## the one thing you must not forget

both monarchs want a *story*, not a ceremony. a flawless wedding is a fine story. a catastrophic wedding is a *better* story. **the only failure state is boring.**

the players must not learn this until the end. they will believe they're choosing between doing their jobs and burning it all down. let them agonize. it's funnier.

everything else in this adventure you can improvise around. that you cannot.

---

## the Ballad Clock

the spine of the one-shot. draw ten boxes where everyone can see them, before anyone sits down.

> **THE BALLAD CLOCK — 10 VERSES**
> somewhere in this village, someone is writing all of this down.

tick a **Verse** whenever something happens that a bard would bother to sing about: a grand gesture, a spectacular disaster, a genuinely clever lie, a moment of real feeling, a goat in a wedding dress. loyalists and saboteurs both earn them. so do the NPCs.

**never tell them what it measures.** loyalists read it as a ruin-meter and panic when it fills. saboteurs read it as a win-meter and panic when it doesn't. both wrong. both entertaining.

**pace:** 1–2 per Preparation, 2 for the combat, 2–3 in act IV. land at 7–9 by the vows. two things hang off the count, so keep it honest: whether the memories come back, and which ending you get.

**boredom rule:** twenty minutes with no Verse and Puck intervenes to fix that, at the party's expense.

---

## the first thirty minutes, in order

| when | beat | what you actually do |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | the gate | draw the Clock. read the boxed text below. Thistlewick checks them in. |
| 0:05 | **the Toll** | index cards. do not skip this. |
| 0:12 | the briefing | three jobs, due by moonhigh. |
| 0:18 | Perquisites | hand out the five cards. |
| 0:20 | **Puck** | pull each player aside for 90 seconds. |

by 0:30 you're in Act II and you have breathing room to read ahead.

### 0:00 the gate

> the village of Mullenwake smells of cut hay and cider apples, and it has not smelled of anything else for four hundred years. tonight it smells of frost and roses, which is wrong, because it is Midsummer.
>
> a hedge of hawthorn has grown around the village square since sundown. it is nine feet tall. it is in bloom. through the gap in it you can see the well, the mill, and a great many lanterns that no one in Mullenwake owns.
>
> a small woman in a coat of woven grass is checking names off a list of unrolled birch bark. she does not look up.
>
> "staff or guest?"

### 0:05 the Toll

this is the only mechanic you have to get right in act I, because Puck's offer and the whole ending hang off it.

hand each player an index card. they write down **one memory their character holds dear**. a sentence is plenty. collect them face-down. that memory is gone. the character knows only that there is a hole where something used to be.

> "it doesn't hurt," Thistlewick says, not unkindly. "it's like a tooth. you'll keep finding the gap with your tongue all night."

keep the cards. you hand them back at the end.

### 0:12 the briefing

Thistlewick walks and talks. three things must be ready by moonhigh.

1. **the Feast.** Peaseblossom is three ingredients short and one nervous breakdown deep.
2. **the Play.** the villagers are staging an entertainment. it is not going well.
3. **the Crown.** Oberon's gift isn't finished, and Oberon has gone missing.

close on this:

> "anything you cannot fix, you hide. anything you cannot hide, you make deliberate. do you understand? nothing here is a mistake. everything here is *on purpose*. that is the entire job."

### 0:18 Perquisites

each PC gets one narrative permission. once per session. not a spell. these are outrageous and that's correct.

| post | Perquisite |
|---|---|
| **Under-Cook** | declare a dish or drink is already in your hands, and say what's in it |
| **Fiddler** | declare that a piece of music is playing right now, and name it |
| **Doorwarden** | declare one creature is on, or not on, the guest list. the list agrees |
| **Seamstress** | declare you have already altered a garment someone is wearing |
| **Hedge-Warden** | open a shortcut through the hedge between any two places in the village |

### 0:20 Puck

pull each player aside for 90 seconds, or just play it at the table. Puck doesn't care who hears.

he wants sabotage. his pitch is that the only wedding anyone remembers is the one that went wrong. **his bribe is the memory card**, which he reads a line of, aloud, before offering it back for one small favor.

- swap the salt and the sugar. that's all. just that.
- leave the west gate unlatched for six minutes at moonrise.
- put this frog in that boot. don't ask.

**expect the party to split.** that is the design. two loyalists, two saboteurs, one playing both sides. no PvP needed. they sabotage and repair in the same room at cross purposes.

if they all refuse, Puck becomes a recurring hazard instead of an ally. if they all accept, Thistlewick becomes the obstacle. both fine.

---

## the only two NPCs you need tonight

**Thistlewick** is the party's boss. kind and exhausted and organized to the point of illness. four hundred years of running these and never once thanked. play her as the best stage manager you've ever met.

**Puck** never lies about *what* he wants, only about *why*. he keeps every promise in the least useful way possible. he is not cruel. he is a comedian with unlimited budget and no editor. if a player out-tricks him he is delighted, says so, and immediately does something worse.

everyone else you can meet as you get there.

---

## where it goes from here

**act II (90 min).** three Preparations: Feast, Play, Crown. each is a scene with two win conditions, a Fix and a Ruin. **both outcomes are correct.** run them in any order, let players leave one unfinished, cut one entirely if you're short on time. the Play cuts cleanest.

the Crown holds the scene that matters: a PC finds Oberon sitting on the churchyard wall in a farmer's coat, drinking cider, watching the villagers rehearse. he is not in crisis. he has not been kidnapped. he'd like someone to talk to who isn't a courtier. play him tired and enormously charming.

**act III (40 min).** at moonhigh the hedge freezes and an emissary of the Queen of Air and Darkness walks in to speak the wedding vow *first*. fae law is a matter of who says the words. she needs four lines, one at the start of each of her turns, and you announce them aloud. one Bride (CR 4) and four Rimeling Attendants (CR 1/4). Puck fights on the party's side and means it.

her name is **Sela of the Nine Orchards**. she was a Summer courtier once. someone who says that name kindly ends the fight without a blow.

**act IV (40 min).** Titania stops her own ceremony to thank the staff, the ones who tried to fix it and the ones who tried to wreck it, equally. there was never a wrong answer, only a dull one. return the memory cards if the Clock reached 5+, and let players read their own aloud. then the real vows, short and plain and not funny at all.

the boy at the back of the crowd with ink on his fingers is Shakespeare. he has been taking notes all night.

---

## how to end it

read the Clock.

| verses | the ending |
|---|---|
| **0–3** | it goes off. nobody sings about it. Titania keeps the memories as payment for a wasted evening. |
| **4–6** | memories returned. the party are named Friends of the Court. one favor each. |
| **7–9** | memories returned with interest. in four hundred years there's a play about tonight, and nobody is flattered by their portrayal. |
| **10** | all of the above, and Mullenwake never quite comes back out of the Feywild. the party can always find the road to it. |
