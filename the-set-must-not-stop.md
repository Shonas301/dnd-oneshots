# THE SET MUST NOT STOP
### a three-day revel one-shot for D&D 2024 (5.5e)

**party:** 5 PCs, level 3, all fae or fae-touched
**runtime:** 3–5 hours
**weight:** heavy roleplay, one short combat, zero dungeon
**setting:** a field in the King's Forest of Cormyr that is not on any map, night two of three

---

## 1. at a glance

Hyrsam, the Prince of Fools, first of all satyrs, throws a revel in the King's Forest every hundred and eleven years. this is one of those years. forty thousand subjects have come. forty thousand is not a crowd. forty thousand is weather.

Cormyr does not know. Cormyr is nine hundred yards that way, through a hedge of hawthorn and bramble that holds a field the size of a barony out of the world.

**the hedge is not a spell.** spells can be dispelled and warded and read. the hedge is the music. as long as something is being played on the Great Stump, the boundary holds. when the stage goes quiet, the hedge thins, and it thins by a foot a minute, and it does not care whose fault the quiet is.

the party is **crew**. riggers, cooks, wranglers, the ones who hold the ropes. they are not on the bill.

at moonrise the headliner will not go on.

**the secret:** the hedge was never actually in danger. Hyrsam has had a hand on it since sundown and could close it with four bars of anything. he is auditioning a successor. he has been watching the crew all night, because the crew is the only part of a festival that tells you the truth about a person.

**the players will not know this** until grey light. let them sweat the clock. the clock is honest about everything except who's holding it.

### session flow

| act | content | time |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | load-in: the field, the passes, the hedge, the bill | 30 min |
| **II** | moonrise: Damh will not go on | 45 min |
| **III** | the gap: whatever you can play | 50 min |
| **IV** | the poach: the Thorn Stage | 45 min |
| **V** | ninety seconds: the Huntsman | 40 min |
| **VI** | last set, and the offer | 30 min |

**trim to 3 hours:** cut Act IV. Grissel walks over to *them* with an offer instead, one scene, fifteen minutes, and the sound cut in Act V happens because a rope gives. compress Act III to two fills.
**expand to 5:** run the Purple Dragon patrol (§8) and let Act III sprawl. the gap is the best part. it can eat an hour and nobody will notice.

### safety

grief, stage fright, and being judged without your knowledge are all load-bearing here. two-minute session zero: lines and veils, an X-card or equivalent, and say these three things out loud.

- the songs in this one-shot are about people who didn't come back. if that's a hard night for someone, we swap it for a song about a horse.
- there is a large, loud, crowded field in this. if crowds are a veil, the field is quiet where you're standing. done, no discussion needed.
- at the end, somebody gets offered a job. nobody has to take it, nobody's character is bound by anything they don't agree to out loud, and "no thank you" is a complete ending.

## 2. the hedge

this is the spine. put twelve tokens on the table where everyone can see them. coins, dice, poker chips, sugar cubes. twelve.

> **THE HEDGE: 12 FEET THICK**
> the boundary is the set list. no music, no field.

### losing feet

| trigger | cost |
|---|---|
| the stage is quiet at the end of a scene and nothing is queued | **−1** |
| a scene runs long with a live gap and nobody is covering (DM call, roughly every fifteen minutes of table time) | **−1** |
| an act walks off mid-set | **−2** |
| the sound cut in Act V (scripted, not optional) | **−3** |

### gaining feet

| trigger | gain |
|---|---|
| a PC personally fills the gap. any instrument, any voice, any noise. | **+1** |
| a booked act takes the stage and holds it | **+2** |
| an act plays something true, and the field goes quiet to hear it | **+3** |

cap at 12. it never goes above 12. it can absolutely go to 0.

**the hedge is not a critic.** a PC banging two crates together for four minutes counts. it counts for one foot, and it counts every time, and this is important because it means the party always has an out. what it costs them is the crowd, and the crowd is what Hyrsam is actually measuring.

**the crowd is a critic.** the third time in a night the party fills with garbage, the field starts booing, and every fill after that requires a **DC 13 Performance check** to earn the foot at all. failure means noise without goodwill: the hedge holds, the room turns.

### what Neb hears

Neb Corrender (§11) is the readout. any PC can ask him and he will answer, exactly, for free, forever.

| feet | Neb says | what's happening |
|---|---|---|
| **12–10** | "it's fine. steady. like a hand on a drumhead." | nothing. good. |
| **9–7** | "it's got a wobble in it." | the lanterns at the tree line go out and come back on. twice. |
| **6–4** | "there's two notes now. there's meant to be one." | mortals at the edge. a wood-cutter. a dog that won't stop. a Purple Dragon patrol that can suddenly hear a party. |
| **3–2** | "it's whistling." | frost on the grass at the tree line, in Midsummer, in Cormyr. one object in the field freezes solid per scene. pick something inconvenient. |
| **1** | he goes very still. "there's a horn in it." | the Huntsman is at the hedge. Act V can start now, wherever you are in the night. |
| **0** | nothing. he's got his hands over his ears. | the field is open. run Act V immediately. |

**do not fudge this.** the clock is the only honest thing in the field. if they burn it down to 1 in Act III, run Act V in Act III and let the rest of the night happen in the wreckage. it plays great.

## 3. the player characters

everyone is fae or fae-touched, and everyone is **crew**. that's the load-bearing bit. crew go everywhere, crew are invisible to important people, and crew are the only ones who can honestly say the thing that has to happen has to happen.

### suggested species (2024 PHB + expansions)
Satyr, Fairy, Harengon, Changeling (Fey), Goblin (Fey), Eladrin, Centaur, Hexblood, Gnome, or anything at all with the **Fey-Touched** origin feat and a reason to be holding a rope.

### the five posts

| post | job | class fits |
|---|---|---|
| **the Rigger** | ropes, canopy, the gantry over the Stump, sixty guy-lines in wet grass. | Fighter, Barbarian, Rogue (Thief), Monk |
| **the Sound** | the nine bark shells, the horns, the resonance pits. works with Neb. | Bard (Lore), Wizard, Artificer, anyone with tool proficiency |
| **the Wagon** | Peaseblossom's kitchen on wheels. forty thousand covers. | Druid (Land), Ranger, Cleric (Life), anyone with Cook's Utensils |
| **the Wrangler** | talent liaison. finds acts, sobers acts, gets acts on stage. | Rogue, Warlock (Archfey), Bard (Glamour), Paladin who's given up |
| **the Fill** | the standby. plays when nobody else will. this is not a compliment. | Bard (any), or any PC with an instrument and no shame |

each post comes with a **Pass**, a once-per-session narrative permission. not a spell. no roll.

- **Rigger's Pass:** declare that a line, rope, or rig already runs between any two points you can see. it holds you and one other thing.
- **Sound's Pass:** declare that one creature is heard by the entire field, or that nothing said inside a circle you name can be heard outside it. choose when you spend it.
- **Wagon's Pass:** hand any creature exactly the thing it needed to eat or drink. it takes it. you describe what it does to them.
- **Wrangler's Pass:** declare that you know where any performer in this field is standing right now, and that they owe you one small favor. they agree that they owe you.
- **Fill's Pass:** declare that the thing you just played was the right song. it lands. no check, no argument, no crowd table.

these are outrageous. hand them out in Act I and do not explain them further. a table that spends all five Passes in one night is a table that had a good night.

### the Fool's Ledger (DM only, never shown)

keep a five-column tally on your side of the screen. this is the audition. mark a PC when they:

| act | marks |
|---|---|
| do a job that isn't theirs, unasked | 1 |
| fill the gap personally, with their own hands or voice | 1 |
| make a promise to a stranger and keep it the same night | 1 |
| get something out of someone by being kind instead of clever | 1 |
| make forty thousand people laugh | 2 |
| make forty thousand people quiet | 2 |
| take the blame for something they didn't do | 2 |
| fix the thing and tell nobody they fixed it | 2 |

highest tally gets the offer in Act VI. on a tie, Hyrsam offers it to both at the same time and enjoys watching what that does.

## 4. act I: load-in

**open in the field. skip the road. skip how they got hired.**

> the field is nine hundred yards of trampled grass that was a hayfield on Tuesday and will be a hayfield again on Friday, and right now it is holding forty thousand people who are not, strictly speaking, people.
>
> to the north, the Great Stump: eleven feet of oak table where a tree stood for a thousand years and then decided not to. nine curved bark shells are staked around the field, and every one of them is carrying the sound of a harengon step-dance troupe who have been going for two hours and are visibly out of ideas.
>
> to the east, a wall of hawthorn and blackthorn nine hundred yards long, grown up out of nothing at sundown yesterday, and past it, if you squint, the King's Forest of Cormyr, where it is a perfectly ordinary summer night and nobody can hear a thing.
>
> a satyr in a coat made of about nine other coats appears at your elbow. he is holding five ribbons.

### the passes

**Hyrsam** (§11) does the briefing personally. it takes forty seconds. he does not sit down.

> "crew. good. right." he hands out ribbons, one each, without looking at what he's handing to whom, and he gets it exactly right.
>
> "rules. one: the hedge is the music. music plays, hedge holds. music stops, hedge doesn't. two: i don't care how the thing gets done, i care that it's done. three: nobody's watching you, so do it properly."
>
> he is already walking away. he says the last part over his shoulder.
>
> "moonrise is Damh. after Damh it's easy. get me to moonrise."

that's it. that's the whole briefing. **he lied about rule three** and nobody will catch it until §10.

hand out the Passes here, physically, on index cards.

### Neb

point the party at the sound rig and let them find **Neb Corrender** (§11) themselves. he is sixteen, mortal, from Hilp, deaf in his left ear, and he is the only creature in this field who can hear the hedge as a note.

he will explain the whole clock, clearly and without being asked twice, because nobody else has ever wanted to know. give him thirty seconds of real warmth. it pays off twice tonight.

**the detail to plant and not explain:** Neb says "three summers back" about how he got here, and then says something that only works if he's been sixteen for all three of them. do not underline it. if a player counts, they've earned it.

**Neb gives Heroic Inspiration.** any time a PC does something Neb would tell somebody about later, he tells them so, out loud, badly, and that PC gains Heroic Inspiration. use this liberally in Acts I through III. it is the cheapest lever you have.

### the bill

nailed to the side of Peaseblossom's wagon, on a board, in four different hands.

| slot | act | the state of them |
|---|---|---|
| dusk | **Nine Grey Hares** | harengon step-dancers. already played. drunk. will not be useful again tonight and will insist they will. |
| first dark | **Cobweb & the Long Thread** | one spider-silk string, eleven feet, played with a bow. genuinely hypnotic. overran her slot by twenty minutes and is now sulking about being told so. |
| candle-out | **the Bellows Boys** | six pixies and a bellows. one song. they have played it eleven times. the field is not tired of it. the field will never be tired of it. |
| **moonrise** | **DAMH AND THE HORNED DANCE** | **does not go on.** this is the gap. this is the one-shot. |
| deep night | **Mother Bramble's Consort** | four korreds, nine drums, no melody, no mercy. reliable. will not move their slot for any price and don't bother asking. |
| small hours | **the Sisters Ash** | booked on the wrong stage. see §7. |
| grey light | **HYRSAM** | closes every revel. nobody in four thousand years has ever seen him set up. |

### the wagon

**Peaseblossom** (§11) is doing forty thousand covers out of a cart with two wheels and a hatch. she is not stressed. she is the only one in the field who is not stressed and this is unnerving on its own.

she feeds the party immediately and without asking. she knows exactly how many creatures in this field have eaten. she will tell you the number if you ask and it will be correct.

**give her one line of foreshadow and move on:** "Damh's shutters have been down since noon. that's not nerves. i've seen his nerves. this is something else."

## 5. act II: moonrise

the Bellows Boys finish. the field turns north. the field waits.

> the crowd noise drops the way a room drops when a door opens. forty thousand faces turn toward the Stump, and there is nobody on the Stump.
>
> the shells carry the sound of forty thousand people not being played to. it is a specific noise. you will hear it again tonight.
>
> Neb takes his hand off the rig. "that's a minute," he says. "that's eleven."

**take a token off the table right now, in front of them.** twelve becomes eleven. do it slowly.

### the wagon with the shutters down

Damh's wagon is behind the Stump, painted black and green, shutters down, one lamp burning inside. the lamp goes out when they knock.

**Damh, Lord of the Horned Dance** (§11) is four hundred and some years into a career and has played the same closing song every single time. it is called **the Long Walk**. he wrote it for his brother **Coll**, who walked north out of a revel like this one, into the Prince of Frost's country, and did not come back.

**what Damh actually wants:** to be told the song still means something, by somebody who wasn't there when it did.

that's it. that's the lock. everything else about him is the door.

### what he'll say instead

play him big and warm and evasive. he is not sulking. he is being extremely charming about not answering the question, and he is good at it, because he has been dodging this exact conversation since Cormyr had a different name.

his stated reasons, in order, as the party works him:

1. **the bill.** he's under Hyrsam. he's been under Hyrsam for four centuries. it's fine. he says "it's fine" in a way that ends the topic.
2. **the shells.** the sound is wrong on the north side. it isn't. he knows it isn't.
3. **Grissel.** he heard the Thorn Stage is running a fiddler over his slot on purpose. this one is true and it does not matter.
4. **the truth.** he cannot find the song. he has played it four hundred times and this year he opened his mouth at soundcheck and it was just words about a person nobody in the field ever met.

### negotiating levers

| approach | how it goes |
|---|---|
| **flattery** | fails. he has been flattered by monarchs. he'll thank you warmly and shut the shutter. |
| **money, favors, bribes** | fails, and he'll be a little insulted, and the temperature of the scene drops. |
| **threats** | he is CR 15 and laughing. it does not go badly. it goes *nowhere*, which is worse. |
| **the bill / stakes / the hedge** | "and if it thins? Hyrsam'll close it with a whistle. he always could." **he is right, and this is your one honest foreshadow. do not walk it back.** |
| **anger** | works partway. tell him what Grissel said, or that the Bellows Boys offered to cover his slot, and he'll get up. he'll also be furious on stage, which is a real ending and a worse song. |
| **a new arrangement** | works. if the Fill offers to play under him, or the Sound offers to strip the shells down to one so it's just his voice, or anybody offers to take the second verse: he stops talking and listens. |
| **honesty** | the key. a PC who says a true thing about grief, or about a song outliving the person, or who simply admits they'd never heard of Coll until tonight and would like to. |

### DCs

| what they're trying | DC |
|---|---|
| get him to open the shutter | 12 Persuasion, or knock like crew and not like a fan |
| get him talking about anything | 12 |
| spot that reasons 1–3 are cover | 14 Insight |
| find the true thing to say to him | 15 Insight, *once*, and then it's roleplay and no more rolls |
| get him on stage before you know why he won't go | **25 Persuasion.** let them try. let them fail. |
| get him on stage after you know why | **15**, or **no roll at all** if a PC offers to be on the Stump with him |

**he does not go on in Act II.** whatever the party achieves here, he needs the night. what they can win in Act II is the *reason*, and a promise: he'll go on before grey light if somebody keeps the field warm until he can.

that promise is Act III.

**pacing:** the hedge is bleeding the entire time this scene runs. call it out. every ten minutes of table time in Act II, take a token, say the number aloud, and let them feel the cost of a good conversation.

## 6. act III: the gap

the headline slot is empty and the field is forty thousand people with nothing to look at.

this is the heart of the session. it is not a skill challenge. it is a sequence of "well, what have you got," and the correct DM posture is: **say yes, then ask what it looks like.**

### the four-minute rule

anything the party puts on the Great Stump takes four minutes to set up and buys **one foot** minimum. what it buys *beyond* that is what you're actually adjudicating.

| what they do | foot | crowd |
|---|---|---|
| noise. crates, pot lids, a rope run through a resonator, forty thousand people humming. | +1 | tolerated. once. |
| a PC performs, badly, sincerely | +1 | **DC 13 Performance** or the field turns. either way, +1 foot. |
| a PC performs well | +1, and +1 more on a **DC 16 Performance** | the field gives you a minute of goodwill you can spend later |
| a PC performs something *true* | **+3** | mark the Fool's Ledger. twice. |
| they get a booked act to play early or twice | +2 | see below |
| they build a bit and commit to it for the whole night | +2 the first time, +1 after | the crowd starts helping |

### resources in the field

- **the Bellows Boys** will play the one song again. they will play it forever. it is the twelfth time. the crowd will still take it, but the *fourth* replay tonight starts costing goodwill instead of building it.
- **Cobweb** is sulking and eleven feet of spider-silk is the best sound in the field. **DC 14 Persuasion**, or **DC 10** if somebody agrees out loud that she was right about the overrun. she was right about the overrun.
- **Mother Bramble's Consort** will not move. four korreds, nine drums. absolutely will not. if the party finds a way to make the drums *reach* the moonrise slot without moving the korreds, that's a Rigger's Pass or a Sound's Pass and it's a beautiful use of one.
- **the Nine Grey Hares** are drunk and enthusiastic and will absolutely go back on and it will be terrible. **+1 foot, −1 crowd.** funny once.
- **Peaseblossom's wagon has wheels.** this is the set piece. it is a wagon. it is on wheels. it is six feet off the ground and it has a hatch that opens into a flat surface. a party that rolls the catering wagon into the gap and puts something on top of it has invented the second stage, and every fill from that point on takes two minutes instead of four. Peaseblossom will allow this if asked and will be extremely funny about the soup.

### the hijinks

roll on §12 when the energy dips, when a fill lands flat, or every time the hedge crosses a threshold downward.

### what Hyrsam does

nothing. he is in the crowd. he is always in the crowd. a PC who succeeds on a **DC 25 Perception** check spots him watching the Stump during a fill, not moving, not laughing, arms folded.

if the party asks him for help he says "you're crew," cheerfully, and gets a drink. **he does not lift a finger all night until §10.** this will read as callous. it is meant to.

## 7. act IV: the poach

**the pitch:** the party cannot hold the gap forever, Damh needs until grey light, and there is exactly one act in the King's Forest good enough to headline moonrise. it is on the wrong stage.

### the Thorn Stage

the second field, over a stile in the hedge, eleven hundred yards south. smaller, meaner, better lit, and every act on it is under contract to one person.

**Grissel Mawkin, the Booker** (§11), green hag, holds paper on every performer in the southern half of the revel. she does not perform. she does not fight. she books.

she is genuinely, professionally delighted to be negotiated with. this is her favorite thing. she will not cheat on a signed deal because her whole business is that she doesn't, and she will absolutely, joyfully cheat on anything said imprecisely.

### the acts she has

| act | what they are | the price of poaching |
|---|---|---|
| **the Sisters Ash** | three dryads, one chord, and everyone in earshot remembers being small. the right answer. | Grissel's contract released, *and* the youngest sister will not leave the rowan she travelled in, and the rowan is planted. |
| **Mabbet Nine-Fingers** | redcap fiddler. plays fast enough to open skin. genuinely thrilling. | he'll come for free, right now, gladly. that's the problem. read the room before you take this. |
| **the Hollow Choir** | eleven quicklings singing at a tempo that mortal ears hear as one long unbroken note. | they must be back on the Thorn Stage by grey light or the contract voids and Grissel takes a finger. she has said which finger. |

### what Grissel wants

pick one, or let them find their own. she takes the first credible offer because she'd rather close fast than close big.

1. **a slot on the Great Stump** for something of hers. any slot. she has never had one.
2. **a true name.** any true name, freely given. she is not picky and she is very clear about what it costs.
3. **something from Hyrsam** that she can't ask for herself. a word. a nod. a coat.
4. **Peaseblossom's stock recipe**, which she has wanted for two hundred years and which Peaseblossom would give her for free if she ever simply asked.

that last one is the trap and the gift. a PC who works out that Grissel could have just *asked* has found the cheapest deal in the field, and has also found out something true about hags.

### DCs

| task | DC |
|---|---|
| get an audience with Grissel | 10. she's thrilled. |
| lie to Grissel | **20**, and she is looking for it |
| spot the loophole she's leaving in her own wording | 15 Insight |
| get the Sisters Ash to want to come | 13 Persuasion, they're bored, they've played this stage nine times |
| move a planted rowan nine hundred yards | not a check. a plan. Rigger's Pass solves it outright and it's a great spend. |

### the cost that matters

**Grissel loaned three of the nine bark shells to the Great Stump.** she mentions this in passing, warmly, twice. it is not a threat. it is a fact she wants on the record.

if the party poaches an act off her and does not settle the shells in the same conversation, **she pulls them at the worst moment**, and that is the trigger for Act V.

if they do settle it, or if you cut Act IV for time, Act V triggers instead because Mabbet Nine-Fingers snaps a string that was doing structural work, or a guy-line gives in wet grass, or the Bellows Boys' bellows finally splits its seam. pick whichever is most embarrassing for a PC. **the sound goes down either way.** the difference is whether they caused it.

## 8. optional subplot: the patrol

thread this through Acts III and IV if you need the extra hour. it activates on its own if the hedge hits **6 or lower**.

three Purple Dragons out of Eveningstar have been camped in the King's Forest for two days, half a mile from a party they can hear and cannot find. **Sergeant Alwyn Cray** is not looking for a party. Cray is looking for **Wend**, a soldier from the same company who walked off patrol last Midsummer.

Wend is in the field. Wend has been dancing at the Thorn Stage since last Midsummer. Wend is happy, healthy, and has been having the best year of a life that was, by any honest accounting, not going well.

this one does have a right answer, and it costs. Wend can walk out. Wend will age a year in the first hundred yards and every year after that on schedule, and will remember the field the way you remember a house you grew up in and can't get back to. Cray will take Wend home either way.

- **if the party fetches Wend out:** +1 foot at the hedge, because Cray's patrol is now facing away from it. Cray owes them. Purple Dragon sergeants pay debts and Cormyr is a large country.
- **if the party leaves it:** Wend waves at them from the Thorn Stage during the small hours. that's all. that's the whole cost.
- **if the party lies to Cray:** Cray believes them, packs up, and goes home. this is fine. this is also **DC 20 Deception**, because Cray has heard it before.

do not put a moral on it. Peaseblossom, asked, will say "he eats. he's fine," and go back to work.

## 9. act V: ninety seconds

**trigger:** whenever the shells go, per §7. best played the moment the party is most pleased with themselves.

> the sound does not fade. it drops, all at once, the way a held breath drops, and nine hundred yards of field discovers at exactly the same instant that it can hear its own feet.
>
> forty thousand people go quiet and stay polite, because they're waiting, because they assume this is a bit.
>
> Neb has both hands flat on the rig. he isn't looking at it. he's looking east.
>
> "there's a horn in it," he says.

**take three tokens off the table.** then start counting rounds out loud.

### the ninety seconds

the sound comes back at **the end of round 4**, automatically, whatever anyone does. this is not a secret. tell the players. it is the actual win condition and the fight is built around it.

it can come back sooner. any PC at the sound rig can spend an action on a **DC 14 check** with tools, Sound Pass, Rigger's Pass, Arcana, Sleight of Hand, Performance, or a good enough idea:

| successes | sound returns |
|---|---|
| 3 | end of round 2 |
| 2 | end of round 3 |
| 0–1 | end of round 4 |

**when the sound returns, the Huntsman leaves.** he does not die, he does not surrender, he walks backward into the hedge with his hand still on his horn. do not require the party to kill him. a table that gets the music up in two rounds and never lands a hit on him has won harder than a table that kills him, and you should say so.

### who comes through

**the Huntsman Rhyme** and four **Frost Outriders** (§11), out of the Prince of Frost's country, over the low place in the hedge where the whistling was.

they are not here for the crowd. Rhyme has never once been interested in a crowd. **he hunts what isn't making noise**, and for ninety seconds this field is the loudest silence in Faerûn.

### encounter build

- 1 × **Huntsman Rhyme** (CR 5, 1,800 XP)
- 4 × **Frost Outrider** (CR 1/2, 100 XP each = 400 XP)
- **total: 2,200 XP**

by the 2024 DMG budget for five level-3 characters (Low 1,375 / Moderate 2,750 / High 4,250) that lands at 160% of Low and 80% of Moderate. **Moderate is correct here and here's why.** this is the only combat in the session. the party arrives with every slot, every use, and full Hit Points, so none of the usual attrition math applies. against that, the fight has a hard four-round ceiling and a non-lethal exit that the players are told about up front. a Low budget would be a formality. a High budget with a four-round cap would just be a coin flip on whether Rhyme's first Winding lands.

designed for **3–4 rounds**. Rhyme has 97 HP, which five level-3 PCs can clear in about three focused rounds if they ignore the outriders, and they should not ignore the outriders.

**scale up:**
- competent party, or six PCs: **+2 Outriders** (2,400 XP) and give Rhyme 20 Temporary Hit Points at the start.
- optimized party: add **one Hound of the Hush**, CR 2, 450 XP (use a Dire Wolf with Cold damage, Immunity to Cold, and Advantage on attacks against creatures that made no noise). total **2,650**, still under Moderate.
- both: 2,850, just over Moderate. this is the ceiling. do not go past it with a four-round clock.

**scale down:** three Outriders (2,100 XP), and start the sound clock needing only 1 success for round 3.

### the noise economy

this is the whole fight. put it on an index card and read it out in round one.

- **making a loud noise costs nothing.** shout, bang a shield, drag a bow across a string, scream. no action, any point in your turn.
- **noise protects you from Rhyme.** he has Advantage against anything that has been quiet since its last turn.
- **noise draws the Outriders.** they move toward the most recent loud noise within 60 feet, every turn, whether they like it or not.

so the loud PCs are safe from the boss and swarmed by the mob. the quiet PCs are free to move and the boss can see them from anywhere. five PCs, two problems, one shared resource. let them work it out in round one and watch them start shouting at each other on purpose.

**Winding of the Horn** takes their noise away for a turn. that's what it's for. save the recharge for the round somebody has just got clever.

### the crowd

forty thousand fae are watching this. they think it's part of the show. this is funny for exactly one round and then it is the reason the party cannot simply run.

if a PC turns and gets the *crowd* making noise, that is a **DC 16 Performance or Intimidation check**, it costs an action, and it makes every creature in the field count as loud for one round. Rhyme loses Advantage against everyone. it is a fantastic play. let it work once.

### terrain

- **the Great Stump.** eleven feet across, four feet up. anything on it is visible to the whole field and has cover from nothing.
- **the shells.** nine of them, staked, six feet of curved bark. three-quarters cover, and any creature behind one that makes a loud noise is heard by the entire field, which triggers every Outrider at once.
- **guy-lines.** sixty of them, ankle height, in wet grass. any creature that Dashes across the rigging area makes a **DC 12 Dexterity saving throw** or has the Prone condition. Rhyme is exempt. the Rigger is exempt. everyone else should be sweating.
- **the resonance pits.** three, four feet deep. anything in a pit is heard by the entire field, permanently, whether it wants to be or not.

### the horn

Rhyme's horn is bone. it has been cut, scraped, polished, and notched three times.

**DC 13 Perception** during the fight, automatic afterward on any examination: it is a satyr's horn. it was cut off something that was alive when the cutting started.

**do not explain this to the party.** they will work it out, or Damh will, in §10.

## 10. act VI: last set

the sound is up. the field, which never once panicked, gives it a genuinely enormous cheer, because as far as forty thousand fae are concerned that was the best thing on the bill all night.

### the horn and the wagon

somebody brings the horn to Damh. if nobody does, Peaseblossom does, without being asked, and puts it on the wagon step and knocks once.

he looks at it for a while.

> "three notches," Damh says. "that's a count. somebody kept a count." he sets it down very carefully, which is worse than if he'd thrown it.
>
> "right," he says. "get the shells off the north side. i'm not shouting over anything tonight."

Damh goes on. he plays **the Long Walk** and it is not a memorial anymore, it is an accusation, and it is the loudest quiet thing forty thousand people have ever stood in.

**+3 feet.** cap at 12 as always.

**if the party never got Damh there** (it happens, and it's a legitimate outcome): the moonrise slot stays empty all night, and at grey light Hyrsam looks at whichever PC has been filling the gap and says "well. go on then." **that PC closes the revel.** this is the better ending. do not steer them toward it.

### the offer

grey light. the field is thinning. the crew are coiling rope. Hyrsam is sitting on the edge of the Great Stump with his boots hanging off it, and he calls the party over by name, and none of them ever told him their names.

> "hedge held," he says. "hedge was always going to hold. i had a hand on it since sundown. four bars and it's nine feet thick again, any time i want, and i wanted about six times tonight and i didn't."
>
> he lets that sit.
>
> "somebody has to do this after me. i've been at it since before there was a Cormyr to put it in, and i'd like to go and be a problem somewhere smaller. you don't audition a Fool by asking him to be funny. you put him backstage on the worst night of the year and you see whether he holds the ropes."
>
> "you've been on stage since the gate. i had the good sightlines."

**let them be angry.** they should be. someone could have been killed in Act V and Hyrsam watched it from the crowd with a drink.

he will not apologize. he will answer honestly, which is worse, and the honest answer is: he was ten feet from the fight the whole time, he'd have stepped in before anyone died, and **he genuinely does not know whether that's true**, because he's never been in a position to find out. do not let him off the hook and do not turn him into a villain. he is the oldest joke in the world and he has stopped being able to tell when he's the punchline.

then he offers the post to the PC with the highest Fool's Ledger tally.
